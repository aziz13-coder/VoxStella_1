Summary of Morin's "Astrologia Gallica" Book 21
This 17th-century text by Jean-Baptiste Morin presents a systematic reformation of astrological interpretation. Morin argues that earlier astrologers relied on either unfounded principles (like decans and faces) or misunderstood natural analogies (universal significators).
Core Thesis: Celestial bodies are universal causes that become specific only through their determinations - their positions in and rulerships over the houses of an individual horoscope. Without these specific determinations, planets cannot indicate particular events or qualities.
Key Arguments:
•	Planets have essential natures (formal determinations) but are indifferent to individual circumstances
•	The "universal significator" approach (Sun = father, Jupiter = wealth, etc.) is too general and often wrong
•	Planets must be tied to specific houses through location, rulership, or aspect to have predictive value
•	The entire horoscope doesn't influence every effect - only relevant house rulers and occupants matter
Methods for Making Determinations
Primary Determinations (Strongest to Weakest):
1.	Location in Houses (Most Powerful)
o	A planet in a house directly influences that house's affairs
o	Example: Mars in 7th house → conflicts, litigation in partnerships
2.	Rulership of Houses
o	The ruler of a house influences that house even if located elsewhere
o	Location takes precedence over rulership
o	Example: If Venus rules 7th but is in 2nd → marriage brings money
3.	Aspects to House Cusps or Planets
o	Trine, sextile = benefic influence
o	Square, opposition = malefic influence
o	Conjunction depends on planet's nature
4.	Exaltation and Triplicity
o	Secondary dignities provide moderate influence
o	Weaker than domicile but still significant
Evaluation Process (Chapter XII - "Principal Points"):
For any planet, assess in this order:
1.	Nature: Is it benefic (Jupiter, Venus) or malefic (Saturn, Mars)?
2.	Celestial State:
o	In domicile/exaltation = strong, acts according to pure nature
o	In exile/fall = weakened, vitiated
o	Peregrine = intermediate
o	Direct/retrograde, fast/slow motion
o	Oriental/occidental to Sun and Moon
o	Aspects from other planets
3.	Terrestrial State (House Determinations):
o	Which house does it occupy?
o	Which house(s) does it rule?
o	What aspects does it make to other houses?
4.	Analogies:
o	Does the planet's nature match the house meaning?
o	Jupiter in 2nd (natural affinity for wealth) = very strong
o	Saturn in 10th (contrary to honors) = difficult
5.	Ruler Condition (if not in own sign):
o	Evaluate the ruler's nature, state, and position
o	The secondary ruler modifies the planet's expression
Synthesis Rules:
When Multiple Factors Apply:
•	Benefic planet + good state + fortunate house = grants good easily
•	Malefic planet + bad state + unfortunate house = worst outcomes
•	Mixed conditions require weighing the strongest factor
•	Application (approaching aspect) > Separation (departing aspect)
•	Partile (exact) aspects > wide orbs
Combining House Meanings:
•	Ruler of House A in House B = combine both meanings
•	Example: Ruler of 10th in 7th → career through partnerships/marriage
•	The house of location takes precedence
Resolving Contradictions:
•	Compare planetary strength (dignity, aspects, angularity)
•	The stronger planet's testimony prevails
•	Benefic following malefic = evil turns to good eventually
•	Malefic following benefic = good turns to difficulty
Aspects Analysis (Chapter XI):
Evaluate aspects by:
•	Dexter (preceding) vs sinister (following) - context dependent
•	Application vs separation
•	Nature of aspect (trine best benefic, opposition worst malefic)
•	Celestial state of aspecting planet
•	House placement of both planets
•	Whether rulers are involved
Special Considerations:
•	Angular houses = most powerful, lasting effects
•	Succedent houses = moderate, stable effects
•	Cadent houses = weak, changeable effects
•	Fixed signs = permanent conditions
•	Cardinal/mutable signs = changing conditions

Deep Dive: Morin's Key Arguments
1. The Critique of Universal Significators
The Traditional Approach (What Morin Rejects):
Pre-Morin astrologers used essential analogies:
•	Sun = father, husband, kings, honor, health
•	Moon = mother, wife, common people, morals
•	Jupiter = wealth
•	Mercury = mental qualities
•	Saturn = father (in night charts)
They would then judge these matters from the planet's condition alone, regardless of house placement.
Morin's Refutation:
Logical Argument: If the Sun universally signifies "father," then:
•	Every child born when Sun is well-placed would have a fortunate father
•	Every child born when Sun is afflicted would have an unfortunate father
•	This would apply globally for days at a time (since celestial state changes slowly)
•	This is absurd and contradicts experience
His Personal Example: Morin's own horoscope demonstrates the problem:
•	Born during daytime (Sun should signify father)
•	Sun conjunct Jupiter in 12th house
•	Traditional reading: fortunate father
•	Reality: Cardinal Richelieu became his secret enemy (12th = hidden enemies)
•	His actual father never harmed him
The Moon ruled his 4th house (parents), was in Pisces in 12th, separating from Saturn - this correctly showed his mother's dislike.
Core Insight: The Sun's essential nature (solar, regal, paternal) is universal and indifferent. It doesn't specifically mean "father" any more than "husband," "king," or "profession" unless determined to do so by house position or rulership.
Why This Matters:
Planets have potential analogies but need actualization through house connections:
•	Sun's nature suggests: prominence, authority, vitality, honor
•	But which of these manifests depends on which house it occupies or rules
•	Sun in 10th → honors/career
•	Sun in 7th → prominent spouse/open enemies
•	Sun in 4th → father/inheritance
2. The Necessity of Specific Determinations
The Central Principle:
"No planet can cause or indicate anything in the horoscope except according to these methods of determination"
Three types of determination:
1.	Local - physical presence in a house
2.	Rulership - governing the sign on a house cusp
3.	Aspectual - angular relationship to house cusps or planets
Why Determinations Are Necessary:
Ontological Reasoning:
•	Celestial bodies are universal causes (affect all sublunary things)
•	Universal causes are indifferent to particular effects
•	To produce a particular effect in a particular individual, a particularizing factor is needed
•	Houses provide this particularization by representing different life areas
The Mathematical Analogy: Think of it as a function:
•	Planet's nature = universal quality (e.g., Jupiter = expansion, abundance)
•	House = domain of application
•	Determination = assigns the quality to a specific domain
•	Jupiter × 2nd house = wealth
•	Jupiter × 7th house = fortunate marriage
•	Jupiter × 9th house = religious devotion or long journeys
Without the house multiplication, Jupiter just means "Jupiterian quality somewhere in life" - too vague for prediction.
The Hierarchy of Determinations:
1. Location > Rulership
Why? Immediacy of causation
•	A planet IN a house acts directly on those affairs
•	A planet RULING a house acts through the intermediary of the sign
•	Example: Saturn in 12th causes illness directly
•	Saturn ruling 12th (but in 10th) causes illness indirectly through career stress
2. Primary > Secondary Rulers
When a planet is not in its own sign:
•	It depends on its primary ruler (sign it occupies)
•	That ruler may depend on its ruler (secondary)
•	Morin stops at secondary - going further weakens the connection ("the more the light is bent the weaker it becomes")
3. Application > Separation
Approaching aspects are forming (future-oriented) Separating aspects are dissolving (past influence waning)
3. The Whole Caelum Doesn't Cause Every Effect
The Error of Total Holism:
Pico della Mirandola's Argument (which Morin refutes):
•	If astrology is true, the entire sky must be involved in every effect
•	This would make interpretation impossibly complex
•	Therefore astrology is impossible
Morin's Counter: This is a straw man. Astrologers never claimed total holism.
The Correct View:
•	For the total person (all life areas combined) → entire horoscope relevant
•	For one specific area (e.g., marriage) → only 7th house factors relevant
Relevant factors for marriage:
•	Planets in 7th house
•	Ruler of 7th house
•	Aspects to 7th cusp or its ruler
•	Planets analogous to marriage (Venus for wife, Sun for husband)
•	But only if these analogous planets connect to the 7th house somehow
The Principle of Relevant Causation:
"When a man's marriage is all that we wish to consider, only the parts of the caelum and the stars which have reference to matrimony through the determinations of location, rulership, or aspect concur in this matter, but not the entire caelum nor all the stars."
Implications:
This makes astrology tractable:
•	You can analyze one life area at a time
•	You focus on 3-5 planets maximum per question
•	You examine their natures, states, and mutual aspects
•	You synthesize into a judgment
Without this principle: Every judgment would require:
•	All 7 traditional planets
•	All 12 houses
•	All aspects between all planets
•	Weighted by all dignities
•	= Combinatorial explosion, effectively impossible
Example Application:
Question: Will the native be wealthy?
Traditional approach (rejected): Look at Jupiter's condition everywhere, aspects to everything, complex synthesis
Morin's approach:
1.	Examine the 2nd house cusp - what sign?
2.	Any planets IN the 2nd? Evaluate each by nature and state
3.	What planet RULES the 2nd? Where is it, what state?
4.	What aspects hit the 2nd house cusp?
5.	Is Jupiter (natural significator of wealth) connected to the 2nd? If not, mostly irrelevant
6.	Synthesize these specific factors only
4. The Action of Signs and Their Rulers
The Dependency Relationship:
Core Claim: A sign's action depends essentially on its ruler's nature and state.
Reasoning:
•	Signs derive their qualities from planets (assigned initio mundi, at creation)
•	Aries is martial because Mars rules it
•	If Mars were "removed from the world," Aries would cease to have martial qualities
•	Therefore, Aries always acts through and according to Mars
Celestial vs. Terrestrial State:
Celestial State (universal):
•	What sign is the planet in?
•	What aspects does it receive?
•	Fast/slow, direct/retrograde?
•	This affects everyone born while this state persists
Terrestrial State (particular):
•	What house is the planet in?
•	What houses does it rule?
•	This affects only the individual whose horoscope places it there
Critical Distinction:
•	Sign ruler's celestial state → affects the sign's quality universally
•	Sign ruler's terrestrial state → determines how the sign manifests in one person's life
Example - Capricorn on 12th House Cusp:
Scenario: Saturn (Capricorn's ruler) is in the 10th house, exalted, well-aspected
Analysis:
1.	Capricorn IN 12th → Saturnian illnesses (cold, chronic, bone/skin issues)
2.	Saturn's celestial state (exalted) → these illnesses are manageable, not severe
3.	Saturn's terrestrial state (10th house) → illnesses relate to career stress, overwork, public strain
4.	Combined: Chronic but manageable health issues stemming from professional ambitions
If Saturn were in 8th house, debilitated:
1.	Capricorn IN 12th → same Saturnian illness types
2.	Saturn's celestial state (debilitated) → severe, hard to treat
3.	Saturn's terrestrial state (8th house) → life-threatening, connected to death
4.	Combined: Grave chronic illnesses threatening survival
5. Planets as Both Universal and Particular Causes
The Dual Nature:
As Universal Causes: When cooperating with sublunary agents (parents conceiving a child)
•	The Sun contributes to generation alongside human agents
•	It acts as a superior cause working with inferior ones
As Particular Causes: When acting through their own specific influence
•	The Sun gives light and heat on its own
•	No sublunary cause can produce these solar qualities
•	Planets confer qualities beyond what material causes can produce
Important Distinction:
Giving off influence ≠ Affecting a specific object
The Sun could emit light/heat in an imaginary empty space (particular cause of emission), but this wouldn't heat anything without a receptive object.
For specific effects on individuals, planets need:
•	Receptive subject (the native)
•	Specific determination (house placement)
•	= Particular cause of a particular effect in a particular person
6. The Timing Issue - Why This System is Necessary
The Synchronicity Problem:
If planets signify by nature alone:
•	Mercury = mental qualities for everyone
•	While Mercury is well-placed (days/weeks), all births = brilliant minds
•	While afflicted, all births = mental deficiency
•	This doesn't happen
Mental qualities vary by the minute in births, not by Mercury's celestial state.
Solution: It's not Mercury's condition alone, but:
•	Mercury's relationship to the 1st house specifically
•	Which varies with each degree of the Ascendant
•	Which shifts every 4 minutes
•	= Sufficient specificity for individual differences
The Temporal Hierarchy:
1.	Slowest changes - Essential natures (permanent)
2.	Slow changes - Celestial states (days to months)
3.	Moderate changes - House rulerships (hours as signs move through houses)
4.	Fast changes - House positions of cusps (every 4 minutes)
5.	Fastest changes - Exact degree on angles (every 4 minutes)
The fastest-changing factors (house cusps) provide the individualization needed to explain differences between people born minutes apart.
Critical Assessment:
Logical Coherence: Morin's system is internally consistent. If you grant the premises (planets influence, houses represent life areas), the determinations follow logically.
Empirical Validity: The system lacks causal mechanism or predictive accuracy by modern standards. The "universal cause → particular determination" framework is philosophical speculation, not demonstrated science.
Historical Significance: This represents late Renaissance rationalism applied to astrology - an attempt to systematize and purify the tradition through Aristotelian causation theory.
The Unfalsifiable Problem: The system is complex enough that failed predictions can always be attributed to:
•	Incorrect house calculation
•	Overlooked aspects
•	Insufficient weighting of factors
•	Unknown birth time = No clear way to test the core claims



Deep Dive: Aspect Analysis in Morin's System
THE NATURE OF ASPECTS
What Aspects Actually Are (According to Morin)
The Core Mechanism:
Aspects are NOT direct planetary influences on each other. Instead:
1.	A planet sends out "rays" to 11 specific points in the zodiac
2.	These points receive a determination from the planet
3.	The points act according to: 
o	The planet's nature
o	The planet's celestial state
o	The TYPE of aspect (harmonic or inharmonic)
The 11 Aspect Points from Any Planet:
From any planetary position (say, 15° Aries):
•	Opposition: 15° Libra (180°)
•	Trines: 15° Leo, 15° Sagittarius (120° each way)
•	Squares: 15° Cancer, 15° Capricorn (90° each way)
•	Sextiles: 15° Gemini, 15° Aquarius (60° each way)
•	Quincunxes: 15° Virgo, 15° Scorpio (150° each way)
•	Semi-sextiles: 15° Taurus, 15° Pisces (30° each way)

ASPECT STRENGTH HIERARCHY
Benefic Aspects (Harmonic)
1. Trine (120°) - Strongest Good
•	Perfect harmony of elements (fire-fire, earth-earth, etc.)
•	Easy flow of planetary energies
•	Natural support, no effort required
•	Morin: "The trine is the strongest [benefic aspect]"
2. Sextile (60°) - Moderate Good
•	Compatible elements (fire-air, earth-water)
•	Cooperative but requires some action
•	Opportunity that must be grasped
•	Morin: "The sextile intermediate in strength"
3. Semi-sextile (30°) - Weak Good
•	Adjacent signs (often incompatible)
•	Minor helpful influence
•	Easily overlooked
•	Morin: "The semi-sextile the weakest"
•	Usually ineffective unless partile (exact)
________________________________________
Malefic Aspects (Inharmonic)
1. Opposition (180°) - Strongest Evil
•	Direct confrontation of energies
•	Pull in opposite directions
•	Maximum tension
•	Morin: "The opposition is the strongest [malefic aspect]"
2. Square (90°) - Moderate Evil
•	Incompatible elements clash
•	Friction, obstacles, conflict
•	Dynamic tension requiring resolution
•	Morin: "The square intermediate in strength"
3. Quincunx (150°) - Weak Evil
•	No natural relationship between signs
•	Subtle difficulty, adjustment needed
•	Chronic low-grade stress
•	Morin: "The quincunx the weakest"
•	Usually ineffective unless partile
________________________________________
Conjunction (0°) - Neutral
Takes the nature of the planets involved:
•	Two benefics conjunct = very good
•	Two malefics conjunct = very bad
•	Benefic + malefic = mixed, requires analysis
•	Planet conjunct its own nature (sign/house analogy) = amplified

HOW ASPECTS ACTUALLY WORK
Example 1: Mars Square Moon - The Mechanics
Setup:
•	Mars at 15° Aries in 10th house
•	Moon at 15° Cancer in 1st house
•	Exact (partile) square
What's Actually Happening (per Morin):
Step 1: Mars sends square ray to 15° Cancer That point becomes "martialized" with square-quality:
•	Aggressive, but frustrating
•	Energetic, but blocked
•	Assertive, but conflicted
Step 2: Moon occupies that point The Moon receives Mars's square determination:
•	Moon's nature (emotions, security, nurturing)
•	Mixed with Mars's nature (aggression, heat, cutting)
•	Through square quality (friction, conflict)
Step 3: Both planets act as "partners" Like two people working together who disagree:
•	Moon wants emotional safety (1st house = self)
•	Mars wants achievement (10th house = career)
•	Square = they clash in their cooperation
Result in Life:
•	Career ambitions (Mars 10th) disturb emotional peace (Moon 1st)
•	Emotional reactions (Moon) damage professional reputation (Mars 10th)
•	Anger, frustration between home/career balance
•	Physical: headaches, digestive issues (Mars heat afflicts Moon)

•	Trine aspect

Result in Life:

•	Easy success in higher education

•	Optimistic, attractive demeanor
•	Good fortune seems natural

DEXTER VS. SINISTER ASPECTS
The Two Motions
Primary Motion (East to West):
•	The daily rotation
•	All planets rise in east, set in west
•	Creates dexter vs. sinister to CUSPS
Secondary Motion (West to East):
•	Planetary orbits through zodiac
•	Creates dexter vs. sinister between PLANETS
________________________________________
Aspects to Cusps - Dexter vs. Sinister
Dexter Aspects (Preceding the Cusp):
•	Earlier in zodiac
•	Approaching by primary motion
•	Generally stronger to cusps
Sinister Aspects (Following the Cusp):
•	Later in zodiac
•	Separating by primary motion
•	Generally weaker to cusps
Example:
Midheaven at 20° Capricorn
Dexter Square: Planet at 20° Aries
•	Precedes the MC in daily motion
•	Approaching the cusp
•	Stronger influence on career
Sinister Square: Planet at 20° Libra
•	Follows the MC in daily motion
•	Separating from cusp
•	Weaker influence on career
EXCEPTION (Morin's Refinement):
"If a planet applies by dexter square to a cusp but by sinister square it is passing from another, the dexter will have the greater effect; but if by dexter square it is passing from the MC but is applying through the prime motion to the Ascendant by sinister square, the sinister will have the greater effect"
Translation: The aspect that is APPLYING (approaching) is stronger than the one SEPARATING, even if technically dexter.
________________________________________
Aspects Between Planets - Sinister Stronger
By Secondary Motion (zodiacal):
•	Sinister aspects = applying
•	Dexter aspects = separating (from that planet's perspective)
Example:
Venus at 15° Taurus, Mars at 15° Cancer
From Venus's Perspective:
•	Sinister sextile to Mars (Venus at 15° Taurus → Mars at 15° Cancer is ahead in zodiac)
•	Venus applying to Mars
•	Venus influences Mars more
From Mars's Perspective:
•	Dexter sextile to Venus (Mars looking back to earlier sign)
•	Mars separating from Venus
•	Mars influences Venus less
Morin's Rule: "If Venus applies by sinister trine to Mars, that aspect is stronger than a dexter trine of Mars to Venus—that is, Venus has a greater influence on both the essential and accidental significations of Mars than Mars can have on those significations of Venus."
________________________________________
APPLICATION VS. SEPARATION
The Critical Distinction
Application (Approaching):
•	Effect is building, growing
•	Future-oriented
•	More powerful
•	Indicates events to come
Separation (Departing):
•	Effect is waning, past
•	Indicates what has passed
•	Less powerful
•	Situation already played out

•	Ascendant ruled by Mars

•	Mars at 10° Gemini applying to conjunction with Jupiter at 15° Gemini
Morin's Analysis: "If the ruler of the first applies to the ruler of the eighth, an early death may claim the native, and it will be through his own fault"

•	Death is more "fated," external
•	Comes to the native rather than sought
•	Less personal responsibility
•	Circumstances beyond control

CONFLICTING ASPECTS - RESOLUTION
Example 6: Ascendant Receives Both Jupiter Trine and Saturn Square
Setup:
•	Ascendant 15° Libra
•	Jupiter in Gemini at 15° (trine to Ascendant)
•	Saturn in Cancer at 15° (square to Ascendant)
The Conflict:
•	Trine from benefic = good for health, character
•	Square from malefic = bad for health, character
•	Both partile, both significant
•	Which prevails?
Morin's Resolution Process:
Step 1: Compare Aspect Strength
•	Trine = #1 benefic aspect
•	Square = #2 malefic aspect
•	Trine > square in pure strength
•	Advantage: Jupiter
Step 2: Compare Celestial States
If Jupiter in Gemini (exile), Saturn in Cancer (debilitated):
•	Both weak
•	Mutual debility
•	Result: Mixed, weak expression of both
If Jupiter in Gemini (exile), Saturn in Cancer (fall) BUT Saturn exalted by degree in 9th from Cancer:
•	Jupiter weak, Saturn weaker
•	Advantage: Jupiter (less debilitated)
If Jupiter in Gemini (strong), Saturn in Cancer (debilitated):
•	Jupiter functional, Saturn weak
•	Strong advantage: Jupiter
Step 3: Compare Terrestrial States
If Jupiter rules 3rd (brothers) and 6th (health):
•	Indirect connection to Ascendant
•	Rulership of 6th moderately helps health
•	Moderate advantage
If Saturn rules 4th (parents) and 5th (children):
•	No direct connection to health/character
•	Lesser relevance
•	Advantage: Jupiter (rules health house)
Step 4: Distance from Exactitude
If Jupiter trine is partile (exact):
•	Maximum strength
If Saturn square is 3° from exact:
•	Weakened
•	Advantage: Jupiter
Step 5: Application Status
If Jupiter is applying to trine:
•	Effect growing
•	Future benefit
If Saturn is separating from square:
•	Effect waning
•	Worst is past
•	Strong advantage: Jupiter

General Logic for Planetary House Influence & Strength Calculation
STEP 1: Identify ALL Planetary Connections to a House
A planet affects a house through FOUR primary connections:
A. OCCUPATION (Physical Presence)
Planet physically located in the house
Strength calculation:
•	Base: Planet's intrinsic strength (by orb)
•	Add: House strength value (Ch. XIV table)
•	Proximity factor: Closer to cusp = stronger for that house
Example:
•	Mars at 5° from ASC in 1st House
•	Mars intrinsic: 6.5°
•	1st House value: 6°
•	Close to cusp: ×1.2 multiplier (estimated)
•	Occupation strength: (6.5 + 6) × 1.2 = 15°
________________________________________
B. RULERSHIP (Dispositorship)
Planet rules the sign on house cusp
Strength calculation:
•	Base: Planet's total celestial strength (intrinsic + dignity + aspects + motion)
•	Multiply by: 0.7 (rulership weaker than occupation)
•	Location factor: Angular house = ×1.2, Succedent = ×1.0, Cadent = ×0.8
Example:
•	Venus rules 7th cusp (Libra on 7th)
•	Venus in Pisces (exaltation) in 5th House
•	Venus total strength: 8° (intrinsic) + 4° (exaltation) + 2° (aspects) = 14°
•	Rulership factor: 14° × 0.7 = 9.8°
•	Location (succedent 5th): 9.8° × 1.0 = 9.8°
•	Rulership strength: 9.8°
________________________________________
C. ASPECT TO CUSP
Planet aspects the house cusp
Strength calculation:
•	Base: Planet's total strength
•	Aspect proportion: Conjunction=100%, Trine=67%, Sextile=33%, Square=50%, Opposition=50%
•	Orb factor: Partile = 100%, decrease proportionally within orb
Example:
•	Jupiter trines 10th cusp from 6th House
•	Jupiter total strength: 16°
•	Trine proportion: 16° × 0.67 = 10.7°
•	Orb: 2° from exact, within 8° orb = (8-2)/8 = 75% strength
•	Aspect strength: 10.7° × 0.75 = 8°
________________________________________
D. CO-RULERSHIP (Secondary Dignity)
Planet has exaltation/triplicity in sign on cusp
Strength calculation:
•	Base: Planet's total strength
•	Multiply by: 0.4 (exaltation co-ruler) or 0.3 (triplicity co-ruler)
Example:
•	Sun co-rules 1st House (Aries rising, Sun exalted in Aries)
•	Sun total strength: 18° (intrinsic) + 2° (oriental) = 20°
•	Co-rulership factor: 20° × 0.4 = 8°
•	Co-rulership strength: 8°
________________________________________

Deep Dive: Elevation (Chapter X)
The Core Principle
Elevation = One planet surpassing another in strength due to greater "altitude" or superior position
Morin states: "The ancients were fond of saying that one planet would be more powerful than another because of its greater altitude or elevation above another planet."
Critical distinction: This is about COMPARATIVE strength between two planets, not absolute strength of one planet alone.
The Seven Types of Elevation
Type 1: Elevation by Apogee/Perigee (Distance from Earth's Center)
Mechanism:
•	Planet at apogee = farthest from Earth = MORE powerful
•	Planet at perigee = closest to Earth = LESS powerful
•	Ascending toward apogee = gaining power
•	Descending toward perigee = losing power
Morin's Physical Reasoning:
Analogy 1 - The Royal Court:
"The situation is similar to the case of those members of a royal court who are in the highest favor of the king and, for that reason, have become more powerful than the old magnates of the court and often more powerful than the princes of royal lineage"
Analogy 2 - Proximity to Primum Mobile:
"Planets increase their strength when they travel toward the Primum Mobile, which is the first physical cause to which they are subordinated in their action"
The physics: Influential virtue is celestial - closer to vault of heaven = stronger influence

Deep Dive: Planetary Motion (Chapter IX)
Morin's Core Doctrine
Chapter IX is remarkably brief - Morin provides principles without extensive elaboration. Here's what he teaches:
The Three States of Motion
1. VELOCITY (Speed)
•	Swift motion
•	Slow motion
•	Average motion
2. DIRECTION
•	Direct motion (forward through zodiac)
•	Retrograde motion (backward through zodiac)
•	Stationary (appears motionless)
3. COMBINED EFFECTS Each state produces specific outcomes that must be interpreted "by means of analogy"
The Interpretation Principles
Speed Effects
SWIFT MOTION indicates:
•	Quickness of habits
•	Quickness of actions
•	Quickness of effects
•	Rapid manifestation of significations
SLOW MOTION indicates:
•	Sluggishness of habits
•	Sluggishness of actions
•	Sluggishness of effects
•	Delayed manifestation of significations
AVERAGE MOTION indicates:
•	Intermediate state
•	Moderate timing
•	Normal pace of manifestation
Direction Effects
DIRECT MOTION foretells:
•	Continuance of effects
•	Progression of matters
•	Completion of significations
•	Forward movement toward goals
RETROGRADE MOTION foretells:
•	Interruption of effects
•	Lack of completion
•	Abandonment before fulfillment
•	Reversal or going backward
STATIONARY MOTION indicates:
•	Slowness of development
•	BUT also stability
•	AND durability of effects
•	Fixed, unchanging conditions
Strength Modification Rules
For Benefic Planets
When benefic by nature OR signification:
•	Swift + Direct = MORE forceful AND MORE fortunate
•	Slow + Retrograde = WEAKER AND LESS propitious
For Malefic Planets
When malefic by nature AND signification:
•	Planet behaves "in the opposite manner"
•	Swift + Direct = MORE forcefully harmful
•	Slow + Retrograde = LESS harmful (weakened malice)
Stationary Considerations
Must determine:
•	First station (about to turn retrograde) = different from
•	Second station (about to turn direct)
The effects depend on which direction the station precedes.
Critical Synthesis Rule
"It is important to note routinely both the nature and quality of the planet and the effect signified, for one must never make a pronouncement [about the effect of a planet] without careful consideration of these conditions."
This means: Motion effects depend on:
1.	Planet's essential nature (benefic/malefic)
2.	Planet's accidental condition (dignified/debilitated)
3.	What the planet signifies in the chart (house rulership, placement)

The Analogical Reasoning Method
Morin states effects are determined "by means of analogy" - here's how:
Speed Analogy
Swift motion = like a runner sprinting
•	Covers ground quickly
•	Reaches destination fast
•	High energy output
•	Short duration of effort
Slow motion = like a person walking
•	Covers ground slowly
•	Reaches destination late
•	Low energy output
•	Extended duration
Application: Planet's significations manifest with similar temporal qualities
Direction Analogy
Direct motion = like a traveler going forward toward goal
•	Consistent progress
•	Destination reached
•	Path completed
Retrograde motion = like a traveler returning partway
•	Inconsistent progress
•	Destination not reached (or reached then left)
•	Path interrupted
Application: Planet's significations follow similar pattern of completion/non-completion
Station Analogy
Stationary = like a boulder vs. flowing water
•	Water (direct/retrograde) = changeable but moving
•	Boulder (stationary) = stable, fixed, enduring
•	But also SLOW - doesn't move
Application: Planet's significations become fixed conditions, for good or ill

Practical Delineation Method
When analyzing any planet's motion:
Step 1: Determine motion state
•	Check ephemeris: speed, direction
•	Note if station, and which type
Step 2: Assess base strength
•	Intrinsic + celestial + terrestrial (before motion)
Step 3: Apply nature/signification
•	What does planet signify (by house, rulership)?
•	Is signification benefic or malefic in context?
Step 4: Apply motion modifier
•	Benefic signification + swift/direct = amplify good, quicken timing
•	Benefic signification + slow/retro = diminish good, delay timing
•	Malefic signification + swift/direct = amplify harm, quicken timing
•	Malefic signification + slow/retro = diminish harm, delay timing
Step 5: Synthesize for judgment
•	Combine all factors for final interpretation
•	Consider timing: when will signification manifest?
•	Consider quality: how will it manifest?
•	Consider duration: how long will it last?

The Essential Teaching
Morin's key point is simple but profound:
Motion modifies manifestation in time
•	The WHAT (planet's nature) doesn't change
•	The HOW MUCH (strength) is modified by motion
•	The WHEN (timing) is determined by motion
•	The HOW LONG (duration) is affected by motion
Planetary motion is the temporal dimension of astrological judgment - it translates static chart positions into dynamic life events unfolding across time.
