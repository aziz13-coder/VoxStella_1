A solar eclipse may last from about one minute to a maximum of seven minutes and forty seconds. An eclipse begins on the western side of the Sun and moves across its face to the eastern side. Solar eclipses usually occur twice a year at six-month intervals, although there can be as many as five solar eclipses visible at some point on Earth in a given year. Solar eclipses operate on an approximate eighteen-year cycle called the Saros Cycle. An average of forty-one solar eclipses take place during one Saros Cycle. During a solar eclipse, the Moon blocks sunlight from Earth; its shadow effectively reduces the Sun's astrological influence on Earth and in the natal horoscope. A solar eclipse that occurs at the time of birth weakens the Sun's action in the horoscope of the individual whether or not the eclipse is visible at the birthplace. The affairs governed by the Sun lack strength unless other planetsenergize these areas. The individual's vitality is depleted.

### Lunar Eclipses

A lunar ecplise (eclipse of the Moon) takes place at the time of a full Moon when the luminaries occupy opposite points in the zodiac. As the Earth passes between the Sun and Moon, the Moon is darkened by Earth's shadow moving across the Moon's surface from east to west. In order for a lunar eclipse to occur, one of the luminaries must be in north declination and the other in south, and both in the same degree of declination or within 1° of each other. Each luminary must lie within 12° 15' of the zodiacal position occupied by one of the Moon's Nodes. If the luminaries and the Nodes are not separated by more than 3° 45' zodiacal longitude, the lunar eclipse is total. If the luminaries and Nodes are separated by less than 6° but more than 3°45', the eclipse may be either total or partial; at separating distances greater than 6° and less than 12° 15', the lunar eclipse is partial. A lunar eclipse may last as long as one hour and forty minutes. The maximum number of lunar eclipses that occur in one year is three; there may be none. One Saros Cycle usually contains twenty-nine lunar eclipses in addition to the forty-one solar eclipses for a total of seventy eclipses. When a lunar eclipse occurs, it always precedes or follows a solar eclipse within about two weeks. Lunar eclipses never take place without a companion solar eclipse: solar eclipses can occur as single events. The function of a lunareclipse in the natal horoscope differs from that of a solar eclipse. The Moon has no light of its own; it reflects the radiance of the Sun. When the Earth blocks the Sun's rays, the Moon is dark and cold. A lunareclipse in a natal horoscope frustrates the emotions and warps instinctive reactions. The affairs governed by the Moon are dependent upon the planets with which the Moon is in aspect. Comparatively few people are born during eclipses. Those that are tend to be extremely sensitive to future eclipses if these eclipses occur in the same degree occupied by a natal planet or one of the angles of the horoscope. Later eclipses that activate a natal horoscopebring pressure to bear upon the affairs ruled by the planets and houses affected.

### The Prenatal Eclipse

Because an eclipse has an astrological after-effect, the solar eclipse that directly precedes birth stamps the natal horoscope even if the eclipse occurred six months prior to birth. The first solar eclipse that precedes birth, regardless of the time difference, is called the prenatal eclipse. The prenatal eclipse elevates the house that contains the sign and degree of the eclipse. In effect, the prenatal eclipse sensitizes the affairs ruled by the house in which it falls; subsequent eclipses that fall in this house or aspect its rulers re-emphasize the affairs of the house throughout life. This can be a sore spot for the individual if the rulers of the house are afflicted in the natal horoscope; if the rulers are well placed and well aspected, the prenatal eclipse reinforces the promise of the house. The influence of the prenatal eclipse depends entirely on the horoscope; it merely emphasizes what is there for good or ill.

### Occultations

If a planet other than the Sun or Moon is eclipsed, the event is termed an occultation. The energy of an occulted (hidden) planet, absorbed by the eclipsing planet, which blocks Earth's view, is distributed in the horoscope via the eclipsing planet. The eclipsing planet dominates the house(s) and affairs ruled by the occulted planet. For example, if Uranus, occulted by the Moon, rules the eleventh house and the Moon rules the fourth, matters pertaining to friends and organizations (eleventh house affairs) operate through the home and family (fourth house); change, a Uranian quality, manifests through the emotions, a lunar function.
