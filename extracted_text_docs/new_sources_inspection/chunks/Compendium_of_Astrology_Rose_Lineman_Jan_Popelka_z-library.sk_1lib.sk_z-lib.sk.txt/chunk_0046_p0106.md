Tables of Houses vary according to the system of horoscope erection for which they are designed. There is some disagreement among astrologers as to which house system yields the most accurate interpretation, for planets and signs do not always occupy the same houses in different systems. Most astrologers study several systems until they determine the one which seems, to them, valid. The Equal House system, one of the oldest, is based on equal divisions of the ecliptic. In this system all house cusps bear the same numerical degree as the Ascendant; the signs on the cusps follow the natural order of the zodiac beginning with the sign dn the Ascendant. Each house contains 30°; there are no intercepted signs. The MC is inserted in the horoscope at the proper zodiacal degree (Figure 15). The Placidean system, designed by the Italian monk Placidus de Tito in the seventeenth century, is currently the most widely used quadrant system (unequal house) of horoscope erection. In this system time is adapted to space. House cusps are calculated from two trisected semi-arcs, the time in right ascension (sidereal time expressed in degrees and minutes of arc) it takes for each degree of the ecliptic to rise from the lower meridian to the horizon (nocturnal semi-arc) and the time to rise from the horizon to the upper meridian (diurnal semi-arc). This system breaks down at latitudes greater than 66° 33' because some degrees are circumpolar (do not touch the horizon near the poles) and form only diurnal or nocturnal semi-arcs, not both, in far northern or southern locations. A comparatively new system, the Koch or Birthplace house system, devised several years ago by Dr. Walter Koch, is rapidly becoming popular. In this method, based on time dynamics, the position of each degree of the ecliptic is considered relative to the Ascendant and the birthplace. The polar elevation of the birthplace is included in calculations for all house cusps. Two other quadrant house systems, Campanus (thirteenth century) and Regiomontanus (fifteenth century), both space systems, are still much in use. In space systems, a reference great circle is divided into twelve sections; other great circles that pass through the points of division, the ecliptic and the north and south points of the horizon are calculated. House cusps are found at the points of intersection of these circles and the ecliptic. The Campanus system is based on division of the prime vertical, the great circle that rises vertically and passes through Earth's zenith, nadir, and east point of the-horizon; the Regiomontanus method uses Earth's equator as its reference great circle. Another system favored by many. Porphyry (third century), combines equal house and quadrant methods. Each quadrant of the horoscope as determined by the Ascendant and MC and their opposite points, the Descendant and IC, are divided into three houses of equal size. Houses in the first and third quadrants contain the same number of degrees; houses in the second and fourth quadrant are alike in size. The method of horoscope calculation and construction is the same for all house systems; the difference lies in the data given in theTable of Houses for each system. In order to use a Table of Houses one must first calculate the Local Sidereal Time. The Local Sidereal Time is the sidereal time that corresponds to clock time at the time and place of birth. The calculated Local Sidereal Time is found in the Table of Houses. The MC and Ascendant listed for that time at the latitude of the birthplace are entered in the horoscope blank. For systems other than Equal House, the intermediate house cusps that correspond to the Local Sidereal Time and birth latitude are also entered. The MC forms the cusp of the tenth house in all quadrant systems. The opposite point in the zodiac is the cusp of the fourth house. This is the IC. The fourth house falls opposite the tenth in the horoscope. In the Equal House system, the MC is not the cusp of the tenth house nor is the IC the cusp of the fourth house.
