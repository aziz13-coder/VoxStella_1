### Ephemerides

An ephemeris lists the zodiacal positions and declinations of the planets and Moon's North Node at the given sidereal time at Greenwich for a specified clock time each day. The data in a noon ephemeris is correct at 12.00 noon GMT (12:00 p.m.). Data in a zero hour (midnight) ephemeris is correct at 12:00 midnight GMT (12:00 a.m. = 00:00:00). Ephemerides are printed in editions that cover from one to two thousand years. Planetary data listed for a birthdate of interest must be corrected according to time of birth. Sample listings from The Concise Planetary Ephemeris for 1950 to 2000 a d at Noon are reproduced in Figure 16.

## Page 110

126 Compendium of Astrology The month and year appear above the columns; the first column, headed "DAY," lists the date and weekday and the second gives the ephemeris sidereal time at Noon GMT on the corresponding date. The listed sidereal time must be adjusted according to birthtime and birthplace. Entries for the zodiacal positions of the planets and the Moon's North Node follow across the page in the upper section of the table marked "LONGITUDE at NOON" in columns headed by their symbols (glyphs). Zodiacal longitude is listed to the nearest tenth of a minute of arc, each tenth being equal to six seconds of arc. When inserting planets in a horoscope, it is customary to round off the positions to the nearest minute. The glyph for the sign each planet occupies is entered in the ephemeris for the first day of the month. If a planet changes sign during the course of a month, the sign change is noted at Noon GMT on the appropriate date.
