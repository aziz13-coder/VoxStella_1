The glyph for Virgo always has a cross stroke on the last leg of the "m"; sometimes the glyph for Scorpio has an arrowhead pointing upward on the last stroke. Refer to pictures of glyphs in Chapter 2 in the Virgo and Scorpio sections. In the column for the Sun, notice that it had entered Sagittarius on November 23. Four other planets also change sign once in November; Mercury from Scorpio to Sagittarius on the 15th (sixth column), Venus from Scorpio to Sagittarius on the 21st (seventh column). Mars from Sagittarius to Capricorn on the 6th (eighth column), and Saturn from Virgo to Libra on the 21st (tenthcolumn). The column for the Moon (fifth) shows that this fast-moving planet changes sign every two and one-half days. Notice the eleventh column, headed Uranus. An "R" is inserted between the degrees and minutes of longitude on November 2. The "R" means that Uranus is retrograde. The retrograde symbol is not repeated each day; it is entered only on the day a planet goes retrograde and on the second day of each month thereafter that the planet remains retrograde (some ephemerides note retrograde motion on the first day of the month). Note that Pluto (last column) goes retrograde on the 24th. A planet is retrograde from the date the "R" is first entered until the date on which a "D" is entered to signify direct motion. Direct motion is not indicated in the ephemeris except on the date of transition from retrograde to direct motion. Always check the planet columns from the birthdate upward for a change in motion or sign. To denote retrograde motion in a horoscope, an "R" is written beside the planet's position. Direct motion is not indicated in a horoscope unless a planet goes direct on the GMT birthdate. Some ephemerides list the stationary periods of planetary motion; most do not. In order to estimate the stationary period, note a planet's zodiacal position on the transition date; that is, the date that a planet changes motion from direct to retrograde (the date on which an "R" is first entered during a retrograde period) or from retrograde to direct as indicated by a "D." A planet is stationary on those consecutive days immediately preceding and/or following the transition date for which the zodiacal longitude is the same as on the transition date. No variation of position is allowed, as even a change in seconds of arc indicates some motion. A planet is stationary on the transition date but may remain so for only a brief time. An "S" is used to distinguish a stationary planet in a horoscope. If a birth occurs on the transition date, a stationary planet is marked "SD" or "SR" to indicate stationary direct or stationary retrograde, asapplicable. In Figure 16, the distance in arc that a planet lies north or south of the celestial equator, its declination, is listed for every third day in the lower columns for November 1950. Direction of declination (N = north; S = south) is indicated in the first entry and throughout the column as changes occur. Declinations, exact at Noon GMT on the entry date, are corrected according to birthdate and birthtime.
