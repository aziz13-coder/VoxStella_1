Conversion of Standard Time to Local Mean Time Some astrological calculations require that a birthtime recorded in standard time be converted to Local Mean Time (LMT). Local Mean Time is based on the position ol the Sun relative to a particular locality. It is 12:00 p.m. (noon) Local Mean Time in a given locality when the Sun reaches its highest point in the sky relative to that place. Clocks based on standard time read noon throughout the entire time zone when the Sun reaches its high point overhead at the Standard Time

Because the Sun appears to move from east to west across the sky, the Sun has passed the noon point in places east of the Standard Time Meridian before standard time clocks there read noon; the Sun has not reached the noon point in places west of the Standard Time Meridian when standard time clocks in those places read noon. When standard time clocks read noon in localities east of the Standard Time Meridian, it is actually later according to Local Mean Time because the Sun has already passed the noon point in those places. When standard time clocks read noon in places west of the Standard Time Meridian, it is actually earlier by Local Mean Time because the Sun has not yet reached the noon point overhead. The time difference between Local Mean Time and standard time depends upon the distance in longitude between the birth locality of interest and the Standard Time Meridian in use in that locality. To convert a standard birthtime to Local Mean Time, the longitude differential (distance in arc between the longitude of the birthplace and the longitude of the Standard Time Meridian in use at the birthplace) is changed to time units. The longitude differential expressed in units of time is added to the standard time for birth localities that lie east of the Standard Time Meridian; the differential is subtracted from standard time if the birthplace is situated west of the Standard Time Meridian. Conversion of Birthtime to Local Sidereal Time Before calculations are begun, the name of the subject, the date of birth, the place of birth, the time of birth and the kind of clocktime in effect on the birthdate are recorded. A birthtime given in Daylight Savings Time or War Time is changed to standard time. The longitude and latitude of the birthplace are noted. Printed calculation sheets and horoscope blanks provide space for such data. There are several methods of calculating the Local Sidereal Time. The astrologer uses the method with which he or she feels at ease. The two most common methods are presented here. The student should become familiar with both methods as a check against errors until the calculations are mastered. Method I: The LMT Method of Calculation Until standard time was adopted on a world-wide basis, many localities observed Local Mean Time.
