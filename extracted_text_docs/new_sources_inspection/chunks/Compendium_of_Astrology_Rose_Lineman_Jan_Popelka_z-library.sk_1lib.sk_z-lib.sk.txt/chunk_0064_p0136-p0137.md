### Declinations

The declinations listed in the ephemeris are corrected in a manner similar to that used to correct the zodiacal positions of the planets. The procedure is the same; the daily travel of declination replaces daily motion in the calculations. To determine the correction for declination, the daily travel of declination is multiplied by the GMT constant (the same GMT constant used to correct the planets' zodiacal longitude), or, if the log method is preferred, the log of travel is added to the log constant (the same log constant used to correct planetary positions). If the ephemeris used does not list declinations for each day. daily travel is based on the average travel per day during the time span between entries. Declinations for an unlisted date are interpolated according to the two consecutive entries between which the date of interest falls.

Note whether the declination is increasing numerically or decreasing. The correction for increasing declination is added to or subtracted from the listed declination according to the rules for correcting a planet in direct motion; decreasing declination is treated in the same manner as a retrograde planet. If a planet crosses the celestial equator (0° declination) from N to S or vice versa, the declination will decrease to 0° and then increase. The correction is treated accordingly.
