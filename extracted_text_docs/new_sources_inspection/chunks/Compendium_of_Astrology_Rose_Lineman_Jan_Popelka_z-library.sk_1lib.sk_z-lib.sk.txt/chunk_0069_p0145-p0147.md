### Midpoints

Sensitive degrees in a horoscope, called midpoints, are usually calculated along with aspects. A direct midpoint is that point in the zodiac and in the horoscope lying exactly midway between two planets. When aspected by a third planet it describes by house and sign position a common focus of interest, which links the three planets as indicated by the aspect. There are two such points for each pair of planets. For example, 10° Virgo is a midpoint of Venus at 10° Leo and Jupiter at 10° Libra; the opposite degree and other direct midpoint, 10° Pisces, also lies midway between the two planets. Planets that aspect these midpoints (within 1° orb) influence both Venus and Jupiter, although not as strongly as if in direct aspect with the planets themselves. The conjunction, the most influential midpoint aspect, provides a direct channel for release of the energies of all three planets involved. Any point in a horoscope that forms an angle of 45°, 90° or 135° (semi-square, squareor sesquiquadrate) with a direct midpoint is an indirect midpoint. Aspects to these points give clues to subtle relationships among planets. Indirect midpoints are frequently omitted in a natal horoscope. They are, however, useful in determining future trends and events, as are direct midpoints. Potentials described in the natal horoscope often manifest when a progressed or transiting planet comes to the degree occupied by a midpoint (conjuncts the midpoint). Midpoint conjunctions are most significant when the two planets involved form a natal aspect, as in the Venus/Jupiter example above (Venus sextile Jupiter). A progressed or transiting planet that

### Methodology

Delineation of a horoscope involves more than interpretation of astrological factors. A horoscope contains a wealth of information and the key to delineation lies in synthesizing the various components into a cohesive whole. Some items of information may seem to conflict with others. It is up to the astrologer to analyze and weigh the data, to correlate the findings and to place the proper emphasis on each astrological fact. If apparently contradictory findings appear, the astrologer must first determine if the deductions are valid from an astrological point of view. If no error is discovered, the astrologer must decide whether one piece of data outweighs the other in influence or if the items represent a point of major conflict in the character or life of the individual. Bear in mind that the planets energize the horoscope, the signs color the expression of planetary energies, the houses denote the direction of these energies and aspects qualify planetary energies and direct planetary influence throughout various parts of the horoscope. No single component of the horoscope can be isolated from the others. With practice, every astrologer develops the particular system of organizing data that is most natural for him or her. The beginner usually makes comprehensive notes based on each of the astrological factors and the corresponding deductions (interpretations). One method is to list interpretational notes under specific categories that correspond to the areas of life described in the horoscope. A piece of astrological information that applies to more than one area of life is entered under each applicable category and interpreted accordingly. Each astrological factor is listed beside the corresponding deduction(s). This procedure serves as a check against omissions and other mistakes and enables one to verify the findings. It is simplest to jot down interpretational notes on large index cards or in a loose-leaf notebook, reserving one card or one page for each category. It is an easy matter to insert additional cards or pages as needed for lengthy categories. Suggested categories include physical characteristics, intellectual aptitudes and interests, emotional qualities, career potential, financial prospects, romance, marital outlook, family life, children, friendship, social interests, hobbies, health and miscellaneous activities. Other categories may be added or some combined to suit the individual horoscope. Obvious characteristics of a horoscope may be noted on the chart form for easy reference. Where applicable, deductions based on these characteristics are entered in the interpretational notes under the correct category; then each element of the horoscope is studied in detail.
