### Chapter 3. When determining the emphasis of

planetary action, consider the effect of the planet's motion (direct, retrograde or stationary). Study a planet's total sphere of influence, which includes the characteristics, drives and affairs naturally governed by the planet: the affairs of the house it rules and occupies in the horoscope, the affairs of its natural house; the affairs associated with the sign in which it is placed; and the natural affairs of the houses that are ruled and occupied by the planet(s) it aspects. Weigh a planet's influence in each area in light of the influence other planets exert in the same area. An aspect's influence is weighed in terms of the other aspects formed by the same two planets with other planets in the horoscope. An aspect that forms part of a special configuration is interpreted accordingly. The aspect meanings given in Chapters 16 through 26 are to be interpreted in relation to the affairs or characteristics the aspects influence. Consider whether the aspect is approaching or departing, applying or separating. The house of the prenatal eclipse is emphasized; the eclipse event must be considered in the interpretation of the astrological factors that influence this house. If a natal solar or lunar eclipse occurred on the birthdate, the effects of the eclipse are taken into account in the interpretation of the eclipsed luminary. An alternate order of procedure that may be substituted in Step 11 follows. Instead of methodically studying the astrological factors that influence each house, one organizes the interpretation around the planets. Beginning with the Sun or chart ruler, each planet's sign and house position are interpreted. Its aspects are interpreted, its influence in the house it rules in the horoscope and the signs associated with that house are interpreted, and its influence in its natural house is noted. Lastly, the Nodes, Part of Fortune, Vertex and East Point are interpreted. With either procedure all the information contained in the horoscope is transferred to the interpretational notes and arranged systematically under the various categories. After all astrological factors are interpreted and listed in appropriate categories, findings are analyzed objectively as they relate to specific areas of life. As the interpretational notes are reviewed, the same notation will probably be discovered beside different astrological factors, and this is as it should be. More than one indication is needed to confirm a given point. It is usually easier for the beginner to write the final analysis of a horoscope rather than to give it verbally in person or on tape. Before writing or giving an oral report, it is wise to make an outline that covers all areas of life. The information contained in the interpretational notes can then be easily condensed into a readable or listenable form that is coherent and complete.
