Some people seem to be "stuck" in a phase long after the corresponding planetary age has ended; others advance much faster. Knowledge of a client's developmental process enables the astrologer to gear counseling within the frame of current development and to help that person advance to the next stage. Traditionally, human life was thought to consist of seven stages. However, in view of current longevity and recognition of the spiritual aspects of human nature as opposed to the purely physical (mental, emotional and material body), Uranus, Neptune and Pluto, which are not included in the traditional "seven ages of man," are given rulership over specific periods in life and afterlife along with the other seven planets as noted in Chapter 3. One cannot gain proper perspective by studying only the planet associated with the year of interest. A person's growth in each phase of life influences development in following periods. For example, the first seven years of life, ruled by the Moon, indicate development of instinctive reactions. Reasoning is not yet a tool available to the individual; the child learns by copying the actions of other persons. If the Moon is very strong natally by sign, house and aspect, this person, as an adult, will tend to revert to action without thought; reaction is emotional and instinctive rather than logical. One may teach himself or herself to stop and think in adult years, but the first and strongest instinct will be to respond emotionally. Mercury, ruler of years seven to fifteen, brings into play the ability to think and plan action through rational thought. This period, one of primary learning, focuses on education and communication with others. A strong natal Mercury indicates that the individual will be guided in later years by rational thought rather than emotions. Mercury retrograde in the natal chart shows a tendency to internalize intellectual processes. Since Mercury's retrograde period averages about three weeks, progressed Mercury will go direct within the first twenty-one years of life. At this juncture, great change in the use of communicative and thought processes occurs as intellectual development becomes externalized. An afflicted Mercury does not necessarily indicate difficulty in grasping ideas but it can mean the individual has trouble communicating them and moving into the next stage of development

By comparing the progressed chart and transits with the natal chart and noting the strength of the planetary ruler of an age in relation to other planets (natal, progressed and transiting), one can determine past and present developmental progress and future needs. One's growth pattern is not fatalistic; it can be changed by the individual who recognizes developmental needs. For example, if the natal chart indicates that the first stage of development is correlated to a later age, the individual will probably have trouble adjusting during early life because the tendency is to skip fundamentals, wanting to run before learning to crawl. Future development may be hindered if the individual continues on this path; the choice to change the pattern rests with the individual's decision to develop the stage required. Because humans are complex beings, all facets of a developmental stage do not necessarily progress at the same rate; a person may complete a particular stage as it applies to one area of life while still developing in another area. For example, even though intellectual growth is advanced, lack of understanding (on an intellectual plane) of financial principles may be evident if emotional development (as it relates to the second house) is not sufficient to discipline the mind about these matters. It is helpful to consider planetary ages in conjunction with the developmental focus of quadrant houses (Chapter 5) in order to determine one's stage of development in a specific area of life during a given time period. Generational Similarities and Differences Influenced by the Outermost Planets Due to the length of time each of the three outermost planets (Uranus, Neptune and Pluto) spend in a sign, their influence is reflected in world trends and generational developments. As one or another of these planets changes sign, differences within the same generation become apparent and misunderstandings between the young and the old are created. Motivation and action of a generation differ according to changes in the mode and element of predominating signs. For example, a shift from a cardinal to a fixed sign (mode) or fire to earth (element) brings about a corresponding shift in generational emphasis.
