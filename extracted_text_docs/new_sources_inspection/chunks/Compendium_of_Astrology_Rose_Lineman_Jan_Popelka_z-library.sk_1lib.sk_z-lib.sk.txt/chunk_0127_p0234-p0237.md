### Astrological Eras: The Great Year

The emphasis of an astrological era, a period of approximately two thousand years equal to one Great Year month, is reflected in characteristics common to different cultures during the period. The duration of a Great Year depends upon the motion of Earth's pole. Uneven effects of the gravitational pull of the Sun and Moon on Earth's equator create nutation (from the Latin nutare, to nod), a small nodding motion or wobble in Earth's axis. As a result, the poles of Earth's axis slowly revolve around the pole of the ecliptic, taking approximately 25,800 calendar years to complete a circle (cycle), one Great Year. This complex motion influences the direction in which Earth's pole points (now toward Polaris, the North Star, in a few thousand years toward Alpha Cephei, the future Polestar) and Precession of the

The vernal equinoctial point (0° Aries) moves such that a different constellation appears as its celestial backdrop every two thousand years or so depending upon the size of the constellation since some are larger than others. Relative motion between the equinoctial point and the constellations is backward, proceeding from the end to the beginning of a constellation, so changing background constellations appear in reverse zodiacal order. A Great Year is divided into twelve "months" (astrological eras), each ruled by the sign that corresponds to the constellation forming the backdrop for 0° Aries during that period. The sign associated with an astrological era and its planetary ruler exert broad influence on world affairs and cultures during the period. It is difficult to determine exact significance in early eras before history was recorded; however, artifacts and religious legends associated with various historical periods do relate to corresponding astrological eras. During the early Mesolithic Period (Pre-Neolithic), which began ca, 12,000 B.C., primitives started to domesticate animals and build crude shelters, both Virgo functions. In about two thousand years, these peoples had progressed to the point that religion became more complex and they developed elaborate burial rites, an attribute of Leo. In some regions, cremation of the dead (Leo is a fire sign) was practiced. Further evidence that Leo and its ruler, the Sun, played a large part in history at that time is shown by the discovery of religious artifacts believed to be symbols of the Sun. The great floods that appeared in all parts of Earth are associated with the age of Cancer (ca. 8,000 B.C.). At about the time the Neolithic Period opened, the age of Gemini (ca. 6,000 B.C.) began. It was during this age that the Babylonians developed cuneiform, the earliest known form of writing. History tells us this occurred around 3,500 B.C., which is not too far removed from the probable time of the Gemini age, 6,000 to 4,000 B.C., give or take a thousand years or so. A winged bull, a religious symbol sculpted in stone by ancient Assyrians (date unknown), can be seen in the Metropolitan Museum of Art. The great pyramids of Egypt were built during the Taurus age, an appropriate development at a time when a fixed/earth sign was dominant. People became more militant under the influence of Aries, ca. 2,000 B.C., ruled by the war planet. Mars. Many cultures adopted warlike gods, heroes for whom they fought and conquered. This was the age of the warrior, personified by Alexander the Great and the gladiators of Rome, In some cultures, the burnt offering and the lamb (ruled by Aries) replaced worship of the bull (ruled by Taurus); in others, various forms of fire worship developed. Aries introduced the Iron Age about 1,100 B.C., and the widespread use of iron (and later, steel) tools and weapons began. The present astrological era, the age of Pisces, began around 1 a.d., probably shortly before the birth of Christ, Pisces' symbol, the fish, became the symbol for early Christians. This is a spiritual age that gave birth to many religious leaders. Buddha, Zoroaster, Confucius, Lao-tse and, more recently, Martin Luther, Paramahansa Yogananda, Mohandas Gandhi and Mary Baker Eddy among them.
