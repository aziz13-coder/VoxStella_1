### Planetary Nodes

The nodes of a planet are points in space at which the plane of the planet's orbit intersects the plane of the ecliptic. The zodiacal degree occupied by a planet when its path crosses the ecliptic from south to north marks the position of its north node (ascending node); the position of its south node (descending node) depends on its zodiacal position when it crosses the ecliptic from north to south. A planet's crossing of the ecliptic is indicated when its celestial latitude, the distance it lies north or south of the ecliptic, is zero. The ecliptic is defined by the Sun's apparent path; therefore, the Sun cannot travel north or south relative to the ecliptic and has no nodal points (refer to Chapter I). The Moon's Nodes are an important part of astrological theory and delineation. According to sign and house, the position of the Moon's North Node in the horoscope points out attitudes, actions and interests that promote an individual's development; its South Node is associated with tendencies that inhibit growth (see Chapters 3, 4 and 7). The Moon's Nodes also have a role in eclipses, the significance of which is discussed in Chapter 10. Because they are not so well understood, planetary nodes are not given as much consideration as lunar nodes. (It is customary to reserve the term planetary nodes for nodes other than those of the Moon.) Planetary nodes are listed in most astronomical tables according to their heliocentric positions. Most horoscopes are based on the geocentric positions of planets and the Moon's Nodes (Chapter 1), It is perplexing and somewhat absurd to relate a heliocentric factor to a geocentric horoscope, so for years planetary nodes have been ignored by many astrologers. It is not necessary to master intricate calculations in order to determine the geocentric position of a node if geocentric tables are unavailable. Many heliocentric tables list the date on which a planet reaches its nodal point; one can look up the planet's zodiacal position in an ephemeris on that date to find the approximate position of the node in geocentric terms. Alternatively, one can determine from the ephemeris the time and date on which a planet's celestial latitude is 0°, note whether the planet is moving from south to north or vice versa and record its zodiacal position which is the nodal position at that time The north and south nodes of planets, excluding the Moon, do not necessarily lie opposite each other geocentrically, although they do when measured in the heliocentric system. The fundamental meaning of nodes is based upon the polarity ot the north and south node and the axis that connects them Traditionally, the functions ol a planet's north and south node are interdependent. It is believed that the north node represents a point of intake at which energy is focused and the south node a point of release for that energy. Another view attributes strength to a planet when it is at its north node and weakness to its position at the south node. However, if a geocentric pair of nodes are not in polarity, traditional meanings are not applicable. Perhaps the problem lies not in lack of polarity but in failure to recognize that planetary nodes (as distinguished from lunar nodes) do not bear the same relationship to Earth as do the Moon's Nodes. The solar orbit of the Moon, Earth's satellite, depends upon Earth's orbit. This cannot be said of the planets. (Interplanetary gravitational forces are not considered here.) Is it possible that the point opposite a planet's geocentric north node, designated here as the theoretical south node, indicates a balance point through which the real south node must operate in order for the full potential of the north node to be realized? Accordingly, the natural tendency lies with the real south node, which signifies a source of disharmony or inherent weakness; correction of these deficiencies rests with the theoretical south node. This then suggests that the individual strive to achieve balance by utilizing south node (real) lessons in the manner and area indicated by the theoretical south node thus establishing effective polarity, which permits the theoretical south node to act as the channel for release and dispersion of energies introduced at the north node. A more logical assumption makes it unnecessary to devise an imaginary node. The fact that a pair of planetary nodes does not lie in exact opposition may be a fundamental factor that has been overlooked in interpreting nodes. Perhaps planetary nodes are not analogous to lunar nodes. The distortion of the nodal axis may be meaningful in itself. If a planet's nodes do not lie at opposite poles of a nodal axis, polarity and complementarity are destroyed. Input at the north node fails to be released or utilized at the south node. The south node then takes on the character of an obstruction (psychological or physical) that interferes with planetary action as well as receptivity of the north node. The "kink" in the nodal axis is descriptive of a perturbation that creates turmoil (eased or reinforced by aspects to the nodes) in areas of nodal and planetary influence, turmoil that is "circular" in nature. Thus a planet's influence is felt at its nodal points; the nodal points and aspects to them affect planetary development. For instance, psychologically-based fears or prejudices, or material circumstances as described by Mercury's south node, might explain interruptions in intellectual development that are apparent in the individual but not otherwise accounted for in the birth chart. The key to reaching mental potentials indicated by Mercury rest with recognizing and overcoming south node obstructions. However, Mercury and its nodes are interrelated in such a way that action at either nodal point or by Mercury influences areas designated by the other two points. Therefore, improvement in areas governed by Mercury or sensitized by its north node is helpful in dealing with south node problems. For example, assume that Mercury is in Capricorn in the tenth house, its north node in Aquarius in the eleventh house and its south node in Sagittarius in the ninth house (a real geocentric possibility). Narrow religious views or lack of higher education (south node in Sagittarius in the ninth house) might interfere with intellectual development (Mercury), career advancement, one's position in society (Mercury in Capricorn in the tenth house), humanitarian understanding and interaction in social groups (north node in Aquarius in the eleventh house). The key to advancement intellectually, professionally and socially lies in higher education or religious tolerance. Since action is circular, improvement in any one area will have a positive effect on the other two. That is, educational advancement can help career standing and community position, give insight into humanitarian causes and permit freer social relationships. Conversely, education may be advanced through professional interests (on-the-job training or education at company expense) or through the organizations to which one belongs. The exact significance of planetary nodes will not be determined until lengthy and detailed studies of case histories are completed. However, at this point, it seems probable that an approach that views the three factors, a planet and both its nodes, as three components of a single element, is valid.
