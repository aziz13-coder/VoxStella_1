Preface 
F ORENSIC SCIENCE PROVIDES US WITH evidence collected from 
crime scenes and autopsies. These are the hard facts in crime solving. 
But criminology becomes a softer science as it considers the testi- 
monies of suspects and witnesses, which can vary widely from person 
to person. 
The human element—always fraught with biases, hidden motives, 
and varying powers of observation—can turn the path of discovery into 
a blind alley or a never-ending investigation that grows colder by the 
day. 
In the process of determining "whodunit," from evidence to indict- 
ment, law enforcement struggles to connect the dots effectively and 
identify the criminal beyond a reasonable doubt. Perhaps this is where 
forensic astrology can serve a much-needed civic and legal function—to 
help connect those dots between the crime and the perpetrator. 
Because the stars do not he. 
Follow the cases of famous crimes—some still unsolved in the courts 
of law, some gone cold to the delight of criminals. In these pages you 
will see how a synthesis of classical astrology, forensic horoscopes, and 
birth charts can reveal whodunit, and often, why.

I S FORENSIC ASTROLOGY FOR REAL? Can the sun, the moon, and 
the planets really tell us who committed a crime? 
In my previous book, Forensics by the Stars: Astrology Investigates, I 
analyzed the event horoscopes, also called forensic horoscopes, of fa- 
mous crimes, disappearances, and disasters and presented my 

===== PAGE 7 =====

conclusions on the kidnapping-murder of the Lindbergh baby, the 
death of Marilyn Monroe, the Haiti earthquake, the sinking of the Costa 
Concordia, and many other cases. 
Those case studies illustrated how the sun, the moon, planets, Arabic 
Parts, and fixed stars all provide valuable clues in understanding tragic 
incidents and their participants. 
After the book was done, many questions continued to pique my cu- 
riosity, especially in regard to murder. If a crime horoscope can de- 
scribe a crime, what else can it tell us about victim and perpetrator? Can 
it give more insight into the relationship between them? Can it profile a 
person of interest? Can it really tell us "whodunit"? 
Inspired by these questions, I began to comb through old astrology 
texts in search of techniques that might help to identify killers. I was as- 
tounded at the volume of information that I found. 
The writings of famous ancient astrologers, among them Vettius 
Valens, Firmicus Maternus, Masha'allah, Jerome Cardan, and William 
Lilly, are replete with aphorisms concerning the likelihood of one per- 
son to commit murder. Natal interpretations are even so specific as to 
indicate whether the murder victim would be the spouse, the child, or 
one or both parents. 
As an amateur sleuth and crime astrologer, I decided to put these 
aphorisms to the test by analyzing famous murders committed against 
spouses, children, and parents, in which the identity of the offender 
was known or suspected. If such precepts held true, then they might be 
valuable when applied to the crime charts of cold cases where the iden- 
tity of the offender was uncertain or unknown. 
I have included the relevant aphorisms in each chapter and a com- 
pendium of the aphorisms in the appendix, which may serve as a use- 
ful reference guide for readers wishing to conduct their own research. 
For the sake of simplicity note that I have sometimes used the term 
"planet" when addressing the moon and sun, even though they are not 
considered planets. 
So began my journey into the deeper realms of forensic astrology. 
Aided and abetted by the early classical astrologers, I was able to find 
ample correlations between the aphorisms concerning murder and the 
forensic horoscopes of modern crimes. These techniques give us even 
more tools with which to understand and analyze murder cases.

mariticide: till murder do us part 
M ARITICIDE IS THE MURDER OF one spouse by the other. The ten- 
dency to commit spousal murder is described by the qualities of the 
planets and stars in the birth horoscope of the murderer, as well as in 
the event horoscope of the crime itself. 
In forensic astrology, spousal murder is difficult to determine be- 
cause the seventh house, which represents the offender in a crime 
chart, also represents the victim's spouse. But not all spouses kill their 
partners—even though that delightfully wicked idea may cross our 
minds from time to time! 
So how do we differentiate between the two when analyzing a crime 
chart? The cases discussed in this chapter provide some answers. 
In murder cases, the Ascendant and Descendant rulers represent the 
victim and murderer, respectively. But in spousal murder cases, we 
must also consider the traditional rulers of husband and wife—the sun 
and the moon. 
I've found that the sun as male figure, husband, and father, and the 
moon, with her domicile over the mother, home, and family, figure 
prominently in the horoscopes of mariticides. As a natural ruler of 
women, Venus also contributes to the narrative. 
The nativities of the parties involved in spousal killings indicate af 
flictions to the seventh house, its ruler, and the sun and moon. And the 
fixed stars and Arabic Parts tell their own stories of tragic destiny at the 
hands of one's spouse. 
The essential dignities of the planets—rulership, triplicity, exaltation, 
detriment, fall, and term—also play a significant role in telling the story 
behind the events portrayed in the event chart. 
Please refer to the Dignity section of the appendix in the rear of this 
book for more precise definitions on the essential and accidental digni- 
ties. 
House placement is also significant in the crime chart. The victim's 
significator or the moon in the seventh house can suggest a spousal 
relationship if other strong connections between the parties are present. 
With all of these astrological tools at our disposal, we can narrow 
down the field of likely suspects to the spouse or significant other of the 
victim.

In the following cases of spousal murder, the planets representing

Some well-wishers have challenged the point of my research: "We al- 
ready know that Phil Hartman was shot by his wife! So what?" It's a 
valid point, but they are missing a more important point—that the as- 
trology of the crime supports the facts of the case. 
So, if we hadn't known that the wife did it, we could have drawn 
inferences from the crime chart that would have pointed us in her 
direction. 
Many of her fans still wonder about the untimely death of actress 
Natalie Wood. Does the forensic horoscope of her drowning emphasize 
a purely random accident or the tragic result of domestic violence? 
The reader is also invited to review the chapters on JonBenet Ram- 
sey, Dr. Sam Sheppard, and Bob Crane, murders that, sadly, went cold 
long ago. The crime horoscopes in these cases offer viable clues to the 
real perpetrators of the crimes, who got off scot-free because of mis- 
guided or incomplete investigations. 
Forensic astrology can't indict or convict anyone of a crime, but it of- 
fers us a way to obtain clues to the identity of the killer, and in so doing, 
it provides law enforcement with leads. Just ask any frustrated detective 
working a cold case of the value of a fresh lead! 
The renowned seventeenth-century French astrologer Jean-Baptiste 
Morin de Villefranche, also known as Morinus, tells us that nothing 
happens in the life of an individual that is not revealed by the nativity. 
So it is useful to have the birth data of the victim and killer on hand 
whenever possible. 
In the following cases of Bonnie Lee Bakley, Natalie Wood, Phil 
Hartman, and Laci Peterson, the sun, the moon, Ascendant, and De- 
scendant rulers are connected by rulership, triplicity, exaltation, term, 
or house placement, indicating that victim and offender were known to 
each other and shared some connection. The forensic charts point to in- 
volvement by the spouse in these tragic deaths. The natal horoscopes 
also depict difficult marital relations with the potential for violent end- 
ings. 
In the case of Sam Sheppard, whose wife was murdered, there were

O N MAY 4, 2001, BONNIE Lee Bakley, the ex-wife of actor Robert 
Blake, was gunned down as she sat waiting for him in her car in the 
parking lot of a restaurant where they had just dined. Blake was tried 
for the crime and acquitted, but many believed that he got away with 
murder—especially the witnesses who testified at trial that he had 
asked them to kill her. 
Blake's behavior leading up to the shooting was suspicious. The two 
had dined at a favorite Italian restaurant in Studio City. Just after leav- 
ing the restaurant, he realized that he had left his pistol in the booth 
and returned to the restaurant to retrieve it. 
While waiting in the car, Bonnie (whose name has variously ap- 
peared in print as Bonny) was shot at point-blank range in the head and 
chest. Blake returned to find Bonnie fatally wounded, and ran to neigh- 
boring houses to call for help. Witnesses reported that his panicky 
behavior seemed staged and insincere. 
Blake maintained that a random murderer had appeared on the 
scene, approached the car, and shot Bonnie at the precise moment he 
had left her alone. It was not a robbery or a carjacking, so a darker, 
more personal motive may have been in play. 
Bonnie Lee Bakley's history with men was the stuff of pulp novels. 
Always on the lookout for wealthy, well-connected older men, she 
conned her way through ten different marriages in search of fame and 
financial security. 
After meeting Robert Blake in 2000, she quickly became pregnant, 
but since she had also been involved with Christian Brando, a DNA test 
was conducted to determine the father's identity. When the test con- 
firmed that Blake was the father, he married her. 
But it was a marriage in name only, and an unhappy one at that. The 
two never lived together; Bonnie and baby Rose shared a separate guest

house on Blake's property. 
The couple had agreed to a divisive prenuptial agreement: if one 
partner divorced the other, that partner would automatically default cus- 
tody of the child. Blake was concerned about Bonnie's questionable 
influence on the baby, but he knew that a divorce would cost him cus- 
tody of the child. Was this catch-22 a motive for murder? 
We can determine certain facts of the crime by studying the event 
chart for the shooting, which took place at approximately 9:40 p.m., on 
May 4, 2001, in Studio City, California. 
The Ascendant is at ten degrees of Sagittarius. Bonnie, as victim, is 
represented by the Ascendant ruler Jupiter, which is on the Descendant 
in Gemini, sign of its detriment. Pluto, in retrograde motion, is rising 
in exact opposition to Jupiter. This powerful aspect is exact to the 
minute, clearly portraying a deadly scenario. 
For details on the shooter, we look to the seventh house and its ruler. 
This is one of the challenges of forensic astrology. In astrology, the sev- 
enth house is the house of open enemies—it rules the perpetrator of 
the crime. 
But it also rules the marriage partner, the business partner, and 
other professionals such as doctors and lawyers. The spouse is usually 
the first person of interest in a murder, but we can't assume that the 
spouse is always the killer. So what do we look for in the astrological 
portrait that will show that they are one and the same? 
Besides considering the seventh-house ruler, we need to examine the 
qualities and placement of the sun, which rules men, and the moon, 
which rules women and marriage.

PHIL hartman: murder by wife 
P HIL HARTMAN, ONE OF CANADA'S foremost comedians and a fa- 
vorite on Saturday Night Live , was shot to death by his wife, Brynn, in 
the bedroom of their Encino, California, home in May 1998. Some 
hours later, after calling friends to report the shooting, Brynn turned 
the gun on herself. The murder left behind their two small children, 
who had slept through the tragic shootings, only to awaken to the night- 
mare of being orphaned overnight. 
Their marriage had been in trouble. Hartman, upset over his wife's 
abuse of prescription drugs, had threatened divorce if she did not come 
to terms with her addiction. In a desperate bid to keep him from leav- 
ing, she shot him in the neck, head, and chest while he slept. 
In the crime chart (May 28, 1998; Encino, California; 2:30 a.m.), 
twenty-nine degrees of Pisces is rising, signifying a crisis. Hartman is 
represented by Jupiter, and his wife, the perpetrator, is shown by Mer- 
cury. The sun and moon, as ruler of man and wife, also figure promi- 
nently, as well as Venus, natural ruler of women. 
Venus and Saturn, partile conjunct in late Aries in the first house, 
describe a desperate situation borne of deep-seated frustration. Both 
planets are in the term of Saturn, ruler of suicide. Classical astrologer 
Vettius Valens describes only a temporary benefit from Venus-Saturn 
contacts: 
Saturn and Venus ... promote success with respect to entan- 
glements and marriage, agreeing and beneficial only for a time, 
not to the end. Indeed they cause abuse, divorces, inconstancy and 
death ...1 
Saturn in Aries shows violence by gunshot, and Aries rules the head. 
Phil was shot in the head, neck, and chest. Phil's ruler, Jupiter, is in the 
twelfth house of sorrow and hidden enemies, and his cornier, the sun, 
conjoins Mars and opposes the eighth-house Pluto. 
The sun and moon, traditional rulers of man and wife, are linked by 
dignity to the Ascendant and Descendant rulers and suggest a strong 
relationship between victim and killer: 
• The sun (Phil) is ruled by Mercury (Brynn)

• Mercury (Brynn) is in triplicity of the moon (Brynn, wife) and term

• The moon, ruler of women, (Brynn), is strong by sign and angu-

The nativities of both partners also bear signs of marital trouble. 
Phil Hartman's nativity (May 24, 1948; Brantford, Ontario, Canada; 
5:00 p.m.) promises difficulty in marriage. The sun, ruling the seventh 
house of the partner, is in Libra, the sign of its fall, in the eighth house 
of death. Planets in fall are weakened by being in the opposite of their 
natural sign, as are the individuals that they represent. 
The weak sun forms a close square aspect with Uranus, planet of di- 
vorce and sudden upheavals. Sun-Uranus aspects spell out brilliance, 
individuality, and uniqueness, but afflictions between these two planets 
may also signify a life suddenly cut short. 
Venus, ruler of women, conjoins Pluto in the seventh house and de- 
scribes a powerful, controlling partner capable of creating major disrup- 
tion. The Venus-Pluto combination forms a difficult square to Mars, 
lending a violent and destructive potential to the marriage—one which 
was tragically fulfilled. 
Brynn's nativity (April n, 1958; Thief River Falls, Minnesota; 4:51 
p.m.) features a grand trine between her Aries sun, Sagittarian Saturn, 
and Uranus in Leo connecting the fourth, eighth, and twelfth houses. 
She was impulsive and quick to react, and her headstrong nature could 
sometimes work against her. 
Brynn suffered from depression, and a deep-seated lack of self- 
esteem motivated her to mold herself into a more attractive image. To 
this end, she changed her name several times, even resorting to plastic 
surgery in an effort to perfect her facial features. 
Some of the cosmetic surgeries were suggested by her husband. 
Plagued by a growing sense of insecurity, she was only too eager to 
accommodate his wishes. 
Brynn committed the murder/suicide in her fortieth year, when the 
ruler of her profected chart was Saturn, ruler of suicide. During that 
year, Saturn was on the cusp of the twelfth house of self-undoing, and

the moon was rising in Saturnine Aquarius. The natal moon-Uranus

bles that lie ahead. 
A comparison between the natal charts of Phil and Brynn, known in 
astrology as synastry, sends up many warning flags. 
Phil's angular Venus-Pluto conjunction in his seventh house of part- 
ners is opposed by Brynn's Aquarian Mars. Brynn was extremely jeal- 
ous and felt threatened by Phil's popularity. Brynn's Uranus rests ex- 
actly on his seventh-house cusp, lending an unpredictable and unstable 
quality to the relationship. 
The couple's Marses are in a stress square aspect to each other, sug- 
gesting issues with anger management, and their Mercurys were in 
opposition, making for strained communications. They had bitter argu- 
ments over the demands created by Phil's career and Brynn's feelings 
of abandonment. 
On May 28, 1998, under the stress of Neptune transiting the degree 
of her moon, South Node contacting her Venus, and a difficult Venus- 
Saturn transit to her Pluto, Brynn put a heartbreaking end to their prob- 
lems. 
As shocking as it was, it was clear to the public that this was a case of 
murder-suicide. But when Natalie Wood drowned tragically off the

tragic accident.

NATALIE WOOD: A FAREWELL TO SPLENDOR 
The Infortunes in Angles denote a public death, or a sudden one; 
the Sun there, afflicted, it shall be by some weapon, or burning; 
the Moon, by hanging, or drowning, according to circumstances. 
—Jerome Cardan, 
The Astrological Aphorisms of Cardan 
N ATALIE WOOD WAS A CHARISMATIC film star whose career 
began in childhood and spanned nearly four decades. Her perfor- 
mances in Rebel without a Cause and Splendor in the Grass defined the 
teenage angst of my generation, and the shocking news of her death by 
drowning brought profound sadness to millions of fans. 
What happened during the hours preceding her death has never 
been fully explained, and the facts as presented do not add up. 
In an eerie homage to her most iconic film role, Splendor in the Grass, 
Natalie spent her last evening aboard the yacht Splendour, owned by her 
husband, actor Robert Wagner. According to the initial reports, she had 
gone topside to secure a drifting dinghy, fallen overboard by accident, 
and drowned. 
This didn't sound right to those who knew that Natalie was terrified 
of deep water and unlikely to expose herself to the danger of falling 
overboard. Why would she, clad only in a bathrobe and alone in the 
dark, leave the comfort and safety of the yacht on a chilly November 
evening to attempt to secure a dinghy? 
Was this a cover story for something more sinister? 
An investigation into her death has been reopened twice in recent 
years, which prompted me to see what the horoscope of this sad event 
had to share with us. I cast the chart for 11:05 P'm- on November 29, 
1981, the time Natalie was last seen alive. 
On her last night, Natalie was on board the Splendour in the com- 
pany of Robert Wagner and actor Christopher Walken, with whom she 
had been working on her last film, Brainstorm. The three, who had been 
drinking heavily, got into a heated argument. 
Wagner reported that Natalie had then gone off to bed but had later 
gotten up and left her cabin to secure the dinghy, which had begun to 
drift away from the boat. 

===== PAGE 25 =====

Interesting. After climbing into bed intoxicated, how was she even 
aware that the dinghy had begun to drift away? 
At a few minutes after eleven, cries of help were heard from the 
vicinity of the yacht by neighboring boaters who ignored them. Natal- 
ie's lifeless body was later found adrift, clad in a nightgown and 
bathrobe. Her scratched and chipped fingernails indicated that she had 
struggled to cling to the side of the dinghy to no avail. 
There was a large bruise on her face, which was initially attributed to 
striking the side of the yacht as she slipped and fell into the water. 
Can the planets shed some light on this tragedy? Well take a look at 
the event chart of November 29, 1981. 
But first, it's interesting to note that the investigation was reopened 
in 2009, at the exact time that Saturn was completing a very significant 
cycle in astrology. 
Every twenty-eight to thirty years, Saturn makes a complete revo- 
lution of the horoscope, called a "Saturn return." It marks a time of 
challenges and crises, and many astrologers consider it the true initi- 
ation into adulthood and maturity. 
For most people, some significant change occurs during their Saturn 
return, which takes place between their twenty-eighth and thirtieth 
birthdays. Some examples or this are a marriage, the birth of a child, a 
new job, a move, a divorce, an illness, or the death of someone close. 
In an event chart, Saturn's return to its original position marks a 
time when the event resurfaces, when old information is scrutinized 
and new findings come to light 
In the event chart of November 1981, Saturn occupied nineteen de- 
grees of Libra. It returned to nineteen degrees of Libra during Novem- 
ber 2009, and not surprisingly, the investigation was reopened that 
month. 
True to form, new information was uncovered during this Saturn re- 
turn, which shed a different light on the case and left a door slightly 
ajar for the entry of critical facts that had been kept from public view. 
According to the ship's captain, Dennis Davern, some important 
facts of that final evening were withheld from the original investigation 
and have only come to light after all these years. 
The captain stated that an argument had escalated into violence. He 
claimed that Wagner had deliberately ignored his wife's screams for

Some close to the case believe that the drowning was more than a 
tragic accident. Can we find any reference to this in the forensic horo-

We can uncover important clues to the final moments in Natalie's 
tragic demise by applying the ancient astrological method of triplicities. 
Passed down to us by the first-century Egyptian astrologer Dorotheus of 
Sidon, this method is very effective in tracking the progress of an event 
from start to finish. 
The method is explained in length in his work Carmen Astrologicum.l 
Here is a brief explanation: 
There are four elements in astrology; air, fire, earth, and water. Each 
element consists of three signs, making up a trio, or triplicity, in that 
element: air signs are Gemini, Libra, and Aquarius; earth signs are 
Taurus, Capricorn, and Virgo; water signs are Cancer, Scorpio and 
Pisces, and fire signs are Aries, Leo, and Sagittarius. 
Each sign has its own planetary ruler so that each triplicity of signs 
has three rulers. We can find out what is going on in a crime chart by 
judging the qualities of the triplicity rulers and interpreting the situa- 
tions that they symbolize. (See "2. Table of Planetary Dignities" and "3. 
The Triplicities" in the appendix.) 
The sign on the Ascendant will give us the element, be it fire, earth, 
air, or water. Once we know the element, we can locate the three planets 
that make up the triplicity of that element. The first, second, and third 
planet in that triplicity will describe the beginning, middle, and out- 
come of the event. 
The Ascendant is at two degrees of Virgo, so the element is earth. 
The chart is nocturnal, so the triplicity rulers are the moon, Venus, and 
Mars, respectively. (In a day chart, the rulers would be Venus, the 
moon, and Mars, in that order.) 
The unfolding drama begins with the moon, first triplicity ruler. The 
moon is in Capricorn in the fifth house of pleasure, recreation, and love 
affairs. The moon is in its detriment in Capricorn and forms a harsh 
square to Saturn in Libra, the sign of relationships. This does not de- 
scribe a pleasant evening; it suggests difficulty with women, frustration,

T HE MYSTERIOUS DISAPPEARANCE OF TWENTY-SEVEN- 
YEAR-OLD Laci Rocha Peterson captured national attention during the 
Christmas holidays of 2002. Photographs of Laci's sweet, smiling face 
appeared on the front pages of newspapers and magazines everywhere, 
causing a groundswell of sympathy around the country. 
Laci had everything to live for. She was attractive and popular. She 
was raised in a close-knit family. She was married to a handsome hus- 
band. And she was eight months pregnant with her first child and son, 
Conner. 
Months later, when her decomposed body drifted to the icy shores of 
the San Francisco Bay with the remains of her baby nearby, the hopes 
of thousands of well-wishers were dashed to grief. 
Laci's handsome husband, Scott, initially had everyone fooled. He 
led a candlelight vigil attended by the news media and gave numerous 
interviews. Each time he came across as flat and unaffected by the trou- 
bling disappearance of his beautiful pregnant wife. 
And when he put Laci's car up for sale the day after she went miss- 
ing, people began to ask questions. 
It didn't help that Scott had been cheating on Laci throughout her 
pregnancy. Scott was lying all the way around—to his wife, to his mis- 
tress, to law enforcement, and to the world at large. Grief gave way to 
anger and the desire for swift justice. 
The deception would last for only so long. Scott's mistress came for- 
ward as a witness for the prosecution, and Scott's charade was over. 
Law enforcement surmised that he had committed the murder at 
home. He then drove up the coast with his boat in tow and dumped her 
body into the deep waters of the San Francisco Bay, using weights to 
make sure it would sink to the bottom. Her body, and the body of her 
unborn son, Conner, would not surface for five agonizing months. 
On November 12, 2004, a jury verdict found Scott guilty of

Quentin, where he is currently housed on death row.

THE SAM SHEPPARD CASE 
T HE MURDER OF MARILYN SHEPPARD was a highly publicized 
case that consumed the national media in 1954. Her husband, Sam 
Sheppard, a prominent doctor of osteopathy in Cleveland, was con- 
victed of the murder twice: once by a jury, and once in the court of pub- 
lic opinion. 
It's hard to determine which conviction proved costlier. 
In 1955, Sheppard began serving a life sentence in an Ohio peniten- 
tiary. But after ten years of incarceration, his fortunes took a turn for 
the better. He hired the brilliant young defense lawyer, F. Lee Bailey, 
who retried the case, Sheppard v. Maxwell, up to the Supreme Court, 
Bailey won an acquittal of the second-degree murder charges, and Sam 
Sheppard was set free. 
Bailey's legal career took off for the stratosphere, but it was too late 
for Sam Sheppard, who struggled to reestablish a place for himself in 
society. No longer licensed to practice medicine, he tried his hand at 
professional wrestling, performing in seedy, second-rate venues. 
Sheppard died of liver failure at the early age of forty-six, only four 
years after his release from prison. 
The story of his arrest and conviction for the murder of his wife was 
the perfect offering to a media ravenous for scandal. Their appetite was 
not denied: Sheppard had been having an affair with a nurse at the hos- 
pital for three years preceding the murder. But Marilyn had also 
strayed, sharing intimacy with a neighbor. 
Their marital infidelities gave substance to a motive of jealousy, 
anger, and betrayal. 
Adding grist to the rumor mill was the fact that Marilyn, at the time 
of her murder, was pregnant with their second child. But because of 
her infi delity, the paternity of the child was uncertain. 
The story unfolded on the eve of the Fourth of July at the Sheppard's 
lake house in Cleveland, Ohio. The couple had entertained neighbors 
for a holiday celebration. Marilyn then retired to bed and was last seen 
alive at 12:30 a.m. 
Sheppard stayed up much later, falling asleep on a downstairs sofa. 
Hours later, he was awakened by loud noises inside the house and went 
to the upstairs bedroom to investigate. There he found Marilyn's 

===== PAGE 43 =====

bludgeoned, lifeless body in her bed, bathed in blood. She was barely 
recognizable, having sustained some thirty blows to the head. 
Sheppard rushed downstairs in a panic, where he gave chase to a 
bushy-haired, stocky man running from the house. He caught up with 
the man, and a brief struggle ensued, but he was struck in the head and 
rendered unconscious. 
Upon awakening, dazed and disoriented, he telephoned a close 
friend and then called the police. 
Sam Sheppard always maintained his innocence, blaming the killing 
on the bushy-haired intruder. Depending on which way the daily 
rumor mill churned, other theories included Sam's or Marilyn's former 
lovers as suspects. 
And there was the local handyman, Richard Eberling, who had done 
some work at the Sheppard's lake house in Cleveland. Eberling, long a 
suspect in the crime, eventually confessed to the murder many years 
afterward. The motive was burglary; items belonging to Marilyn were 
later found in Eberling's possession. 
But this was also a crime of passion. Eberling's blood was found in a 
bite mark on Marilyn's body. Burglars do not normally bite their vic- 
tims unless driven by an urge stronger than the need to steal. 
And they normally do not strike their victims over thirty times in the 
head and face. This killing was not just intended to eliminate the only 
witness to the crime. It was an explosion of rage, suggesting that the 
killer was acquainted with the victim and had an ax to grind. 
This neatly fit the prevailing theory that Sam and Marilyn had ar- 
gued that night and that he had bludgeoned her to death as she slept. 
After all, they had both strayed outside the marriage, and tensions 
could easily have escalated. 
Let's first take a look at the forensic chart for the time Marilyn was 
last seen, 12:30 a.m. on July 4,1954, in Cleveland, Ohio. 
In the crime chart, Marilyn is represented by twenty-five degrees of

pies nine degrees of Cancer in the fourth house: she is at home in her 
bedroom, and she is pregnant. 
Jupiter is in the triplicity of Mars, the eighth-house ruler, and com- 
bust the sun; her life is coming to a violent end. The moon, symbolic of 
women and the mother, approaches a trine to a retrograde Mars at

twenty-nine degrees of Sagittarius—within a few hours Marilyn will

If there are connections by dignity or rulership between Marilyn's 
ruler, Jupiter, and the seventh-house ruler, then we may determine

Jupiter represents Marilyn, and Mercury represents the killer. Both

dom act of homicide. 
But we must go further to determine whether the perpetrator, known

of a child by one or both parents. The ancient astrologers left us many 
writings on this subject. 
In this chapter, we will examine several cases of child murders and 
their common astrological denominators. The key considerations are 
the Ascendant and Descendant rulers, the fourth and tenth rulers, as 
well as the sun and moon, natural rulers of father and mother. 
Stress aspects to the fifth house and its rulers are commonly seen, as 
we might expect. Often the seventh-house ruler, representing the killer, 
is associated with the fifth house or its ruler by triplicity, exaltation, 
term, or rulership. 
Jupiter, as natural ruler of children, also bears careful scrutiny. In 
cases of filicide, it is often linked to the seventh-house ruler or the 
moon. William Lilly states that oppositions from Mars or Saturn to 
Jupiter bring death to the children, and we will see ample testimony of 
that truth. 
In his comprehensive work, Christian Astrology, William Lilly lists the 
aphorisms of Arab astrologer Abubater concerning parricide and fili-

Controversy swirled around their mother, Alice, whose sexually liber-

Alice was an attractive, confident, twenty-six-year-old woman who

Fourteen innocent people were killed on the spot, thirty-two were in-

DID GIANNI VERSACE KNOW HIS KILLER? 
T HE FASHION WORLD WAS DEVASTATED when avant-garde de- 
signer Gianni Versace was gunned down in front of his South Beach, 
Florida, mansion in July 1997. It was the final tragic act in a crazed 
killing spree that began on a New fersey farm and ended with the 
killer's suicide in a houseboat docked nearby. The cold-blooded shoot- 
ing was shocking in its randomness, and worse yet, there was no appar- 
ent motive. 
Or was there? Many speculated that Versace had been acquainted 
with the handsome killer, Andrew Cunanan, in the weeks preceding his 
murder and that Cunanan, a notorious gold digger with an interest in 
older wealthy men, may have approached Versace, only to be rejected. 
Was personal rejection a motive for the shooting? Let's see what 
forensic astrology has to say. 
The shooting took place at 8:44 a.m. on July 15, 1997. The planets on 
the angles tell this story in chilling clarity: Venus rising in Leo, ruling 
the tenth house of fame and the third house of the neighborhood, is 
opposed by Jupiter in Aquarius. 
Jupiter rules the eighth house of death and the fifth house of cre- 
ativity. Taken literally, these planets narrate the story of a world- 
renowned fashion designer (Venus rising in Leo, ruling the tenth) who 
meets his death (Jupiter ruling the eighth, opposing Venus) while tak- 
ing a leisurely walk home (Venus ruling the third). 
The rising Venus in Leo forms a trine to a malignant Saturn in Aries. 
Usually considered a favorable aspect, this trine instead facilitates trou- 
ble, as Saturn in Aries connotes cruelty and hostility. 
In addition, Venus is in her detriment in Aries, Saturn's sign, and 
Saturn is in detriment in Leo, Venus's sign. The incompatibility be- 
tween these planets implies bad blood between the parties. It was ru- 
mored that Versace had met Cunanan at a local party and that Cunanan 
had made a romantic overture that Versace did not reciprocate. 
A negative interplay between Venus and Saturn is also prominent in 
the event chart of the drowning of actress Natalie Wood (see chapter 1). 
Venus-Saturn conflicts are often associated with troubled relationships, 
rejections, and heartbreak. The fact that Venus and Saturn are in trine 
here is not a favorable sign: they are in each other's detriment, showing

distaste or dislike. 
A romantic rejection is not outside of the realm of possibility in this 
case. Cunanan was a social climber known to seek the companionship 
and support of wealthy older men. He had murdered a wealthy older 
businessman and former benefactor in Chicago just weeks before. The 
negative mutual reception of Venus and Saturn in the crime chart im- 
plies such a rejection and offers a possible motive for the murder. 
Planets close to the Ascendant-Descendant axis describe victim and 
offender, as well as the rulers of their cusps. Here Venus rising repre- 
sents Versace, and Jupiter setting represents Cunanan. 
Versace is shown by proud Leo rising, and its ruler sun is weakened 
by being conjunct the twelfth-house cusp and also disposited by the

planets on the twelfth-house cusp suggest an impending doom—like 
someone lying in wait for the victim to arrive upon the final scene.

of endings, and the sun, in square to Cunanan's planet, Saturn, also ap-

Saturn is ruled by Mars, which governs gunfire, and Aries rules the 
head. 
In crime investigations, violence to the victim's face is usually a sign

of exaltation of the sun. Cunanan may have felt attraction for Versace,

But there is no reciprocation: Gianni's sun, in Cancer, is the sign of 
Saturn's fall. A planet in its fall is ignored; it receives no attention, like a 
wallflower at a dance. 
This reinforces the theory that there had been some prior contact be- 
tween the two men, which led to intense frustration for Cunanan and 
fatal consequences for Versace. 
And the forensic horoscope provides traction for it. Cunanan

typically sought out older, wealthy gay men for financial support and

Appendix 
1. GLOSSARY OF ASTROLOGICAL TERMS 
Affliction 
A planet is said to be afflicted when it is in a difficult aspect to another 
planet or when it is poorly placed by essential or accidental dignity. Its 
energies cannot be expressed easily and are compromised. An example 
of affliction is the moon in square aspect to Saturn or Venus in oppo- 
sition to Jupiter. 
Air signs 
There are three signs belonging to the air element: Gemini, Libra, and 
Aquarius. The air signs are inclined toward mental activity, communi- 
cation, and ideas. 
Angles/angular 
The angles of the chart are the cusps of the first, fourth, seventh, and 
tenth houses. They define the four quadrants or sections of the horo- 
scope, from houses one through three, houses four through six, houses 
seven through nine, and houses ten through twelve. Proximity of a 
planet to an angle confers more power to a planet or star and gives that 
planet more influence over the affairs of that house. 
Example: The seventh-house cusp is twenty degrees of Cancer. Mars 
at nineteen degrees of Cancer is on the cusp. Since Mars is angular, it 
has dominion over the seventh house. Angularity also gives power to a 
planet even if it is already inside the house, such as Mars at twenty-five 
degrees Cancer in this example. 
Applying aspect 
A planet that is approaching the degree of another planet by aspect is 
said to be applying to that aspect. 
Examples: The moon at twelve degrees of Taurus applies to a square 
of Venus at fifteen degrees of Leo. The sun at seventeen degrees Libra 
applies to a conjunction with Saturn at nineteen degrees of Libra. 

===== PAGE 148 =====

Arabic Parts 
The Arabic Parts signify areas of the zodiac found by calculating the 
distances between house cusps and planets related to specific inquiries. 
The rulers of these Parts are often of significance in the chart. The parts 
were developed in antiquity but are useful even today, and a part can be 
calculated for just about anything by adding and subtracting degrees of 
planets and cusps on the 360-degree wheel. Here are the formulas for 
more commonly used parts, many of which appear in this book: 
Part of Accident: Ascendant + Saturn - Mars 
Part of Catastrophe: Ascendant + Uranus - the sun 
Part of Danger: Ascendant + Mercury - Saturn 
Part of Fortune: Ascendant + the moon - the sun 
Part of Peril: Ascendant + Eighth Cusp - Saturn 
Part of Plane Crash: Ascendant + Ninth Cusp - Pluto 
Part of Spirit: Ascendant + the sun - the moon 
For a more comprehensive list, see "5. Arabic Parts." 
Ascendant 
Also called "rising sign," the Ascendant is the easternmost point on the 
horoscope and is the cusp of the first house of the chart. In a person, it 
represents the physical appearance, health, and personality. In an event 
chart, the Ascendant shows the event in question, and in a crime chart, 
it shows the victim of the crime. The ruler of the Ascendant, its digni- 
ties, and its placement yield a great deal of information in the crime 
charts of missing or murdered persons. 
Aspect 
A planet aspects another planet when it forms a relationship by quality, 
element, or triplicity. The main aspects are conjunction, sextile, square, 
trine, and opposition. 
Benefic 
A helpful or beneficial planet, usually Venus or Jupiter, if well placed in 
a chart. 

===== PAGE 149 =====

Cardinal signs 
The cardinal (also called movable) signs are Aries, Cancer, Libra, and 
Capricorn. See also: Fixed signs and Mutable signs. 
Conjunction 
When one planet occupies the same sign of another planet within an 
orb of five degrees or less. Mercury at thirteen degrees Taurus ap- 
proaches conjunction with Mars at sixteen degrees Taurus. 
Critical degrees—see Degrees 
Cusp 
A line that divides one house of a horoscope from another, which will 
always show the degree of a sign. 
Degrees 
See also section 6 of this appendix—"Critical Degrees." 
All horoscopes are 360-degree circles, so one degree is of the 
circle. A degree may or may not be occupied by a planet, star, or other 
celestial body. Ancient astrologers attributed various qualities to specific 
degrees of the zodiac, and these are called "critical degrees." 
The twenty-ninth degree of any sign is considered critical, or fateful, 
because it denotes something about to change or come to an end. 
The zero degree indicates the change or beginning of a situation, 
something new coming to light. 
The zero degree of any cardinal sign is referred to as "Aries point," 
which in modern astrology relates to a public persona of the native, 
one's place in the world. 
Critical degrees are often found in the crime charts and event charts 
of disasters in this book. 
Other critical degrees are the following: four and seventeen of muta- 
ble signs (Gemini, Virgo, Sagittarius, Pisces); zero, thirteen, and twen- 
ty-six of cardinal signs (Aries, Cancer, Libra, Capricorn); and eight, 
nine, twenty-one, and twenty-two of fixed signs (Taurus, Leo, Scorpio, 
Aquarius). 

===== PAGE 150 =====

Eighteen and nineteen degrees of Aries/Libra are considered "de- 
grees of homicide" by astrologer Edna Rowland. 
Twenty-two degrees of any sign are considered as traumatic or fate- 
ful, as seen in some of the events described in this book. 
Descendant 
The seventh-house cusp is always opposite the Ascendant and in the 
opposite degree and sign. This describes a spouse, significant other, 
business partner, doctor, client, enemy known to the person, perpe- 
trator of a crime. 
Detriment 
A planet is in detriment when it occupies the sign opposite the sign that 
it rules. Venus rules Libra so is in her detriment in Aries. Planets in 
their detriment do not operate well with their basic natural energies, be- 
cause they are not in a comfortable environment within which to do so. 
Planets in detriment are disorganized. The easy, peace-loving energy 
of Venus is challenged when placed in Mars's fiery sign Aries, so she is 
in her detriment and can't function as she normally would; she be- 
comes aggressive and demanding. 
In crime charts and event charts, ruling planets in detriment show a 
loss of control and an inability to help themselves out of difficult situa- 
tions. 
Dignity 
Dignity is classified as either essential or accidental. It describes the 
condition of a planet and how well its energies function at a given posi- 
tion. 
Planets in their own signs of rulership or exaltation are said to be 
essentially dignified and have power in a chart and control over a situ- 
ation. For example, Saturn rules Aquarius and is dignified in that sign 
as it works well through the energies of Aquarius. 
Planets are also dignified by house placement or in relationship to 
other planets. They are said to be in their "joy" in certain houses and 
are thus accidentally dignified. Here is the chart of houses of the plan- 
etary joys; 

===== PAGE 151 =====

The sun—ninth house 
The moon—seventh house 
Mercury—first house 
Venus—fifth house 
Mars - sixth house 
Jupiter—eleventh house 
Saturn—twelfth house 
On the other hand, planets in their detriment or fall are not well 
dignified because their energies do not operate well within the confines 
of those signs. The peace-loving, pleasure-seeking Venus becomes ag- 
gressive and domineering in Mars's sign Aries, where she is in detri- 
ment, or in Scorpio, where she is in fall. In summary, planets are acci- 
dentally dignified by house placement and essentially dignified by sign 
placement. 
Planets in each other's rulership, triplicity, exaltation, detriment, fall, 
or term are involved with each other; they share something in common. 
When the planets share some connection, so do the people that they 
represent. 
If a planet is ruled by another, it is enamored of the other or con- 
trolled by the other. It is in the other's dominion and under its influ- 
ence. This is often seen in cases of mariticide, where one partner's 
planet rules the sign that the other's planet occupies. 
A planet in the triplicity of another planet is familiar with that planet 
and there is some affinity or relationship between the two. For example, 
I am acquainted with my neighbor; we may not be close friends, but we 
are not strangers. We belong to the same neighborhood. 
If a planet is in the exaltation of another planet, it holds in high re- 
gard whatever is represented by that planet. 
Exaltation is a position of strength, but the planet does not have the 
same element of control as a planet that rules another planet—it is an 
honor given by another. 
For example, I may visit Denmark and be treated like a queen, but 
I'm neither Danish nor a queen, and it's not my home country. An ex- 
alted planet can also be overestimated—I may make the celebrity A list 
in Denmark, but back home, I am just an average everyday citizen! 
A planet in the term of another planet has something in common 

===== PAGE 152 =====

with that planet; the planets share a mutual interest. If your planet and 
mine are both in the term of Jupiter, then we may share common inter- 
est in philosophy, publishing, or higher education. 
Planets in detriment are weakened, as they do not function well. So 
are the individuals that planets in detriment represent. Planets in fall 
are also weakened and have little power to influence matters. They are 
in the background of the action, as if ignored by stronger players in the 
planetary scheme.

rulerships, triplicities, terms, and dignities. 
Dispositor 
A dispositor is a planet that rules the sign that another planet is in. For 
example, if Jupiter is in Gemini, Mercury is the dispositor of Jupiter be- 
cause Mercury rules Gemini. 
Eclipse 
An eclipse involves the parallel conjunction of the earth, the sun, and 
the moon. Specific degrees of an eclipse are very important in predic- 
tive and event astrology. Solar eclipses occur during the new moon, and 
lunar eclipses occur during the full moon. See the article on the Haiti 
earthquake for discussion on eclipse points in relation to historic 
events. 
Elements

earth, air, water. There are three signs of each element, as follows: 
Aries—Fire

By studying the sequence of signs, you can see the progression of

has specific qualities and forms what is called a "triplicity" of the signs. 
The fiery triplicity is Aries, Leo, Sagittarius; the earthy triplicity is 
Taurus, Virgo, Capricorn; the airy triplicity is Gemini, Libra, Aquarius;

Ele vated/el e vation 
A planet is elevated when it is above the horizon, and especially so 
when conjoined to the Midheaven (tenth-house cusp). 
Exaltation 
A planet is well placed when in the sign of its exaltation, which confers 
some degree of power to it. When representing a person in an event 
chart, a planet in its exaltation is powerful, sometimes undeservedly so. 
A king may be exalted because he is the ruler of a country, but that 
doesn't always mean he deserves the high esteem that he receives. It is 
power conferred on him by others. 
Fixed signs 
The fixed signs are Taurus, Leo, Scorpio, and Aquarius. General charac- 
teristics of these signs are steadfastness, durability, firmness, and resis- 
tance to change. 
Fixed stars 
A fixed star is a celestial body that generates its own light, unlike a plan- 
et, which is visible by way of reflected light. Fixed stars are so called be- 
cause they progress extremely slowly through the zodiac, moving only 
about one degree of arc approximately every seventy-two years. 
Most of the fixed stars form constellations and have been known to 
man since the early days of stargazing thousands of years ago. Many 

===== PAGE 154 =====

fixed stars are associated with mythical stories and persons. They were 
considered to have a generally malefic influence, but some fixed stars 
confer great destiny when well placed. 
Grand cross or grand square 
A configuration of four planets that form a shape of a cross, which can 
only occur in planets of the same quality. 
For example: Mercury in Pisces opposes Mars in Virgo, while the 
sun in Sagittarius opposes Saturn in Gemini. This forms a grand cross, 
or square, in mutable signs. This aspect is a very stressful aspect but a 
very dynamic one, as many energies are in conflict with one another. 
The orb of influence may range from three to seven degrees. 
Grand trine 
A formation of three planets of the same element that form a 120- 
degree angle. A grand trine in antiquity was thought to bring ease and 
harmony to the individual but was not necessarily always favorable, de- 
pending on the dignities of the planets involved. 
For example, Mars at fifteen Libra trines Jupiter at seventeen Gem- 
ini, which also trines Saturn at fourteen Aquarius. This is an example 
of a grand trine in the air element. The orb of influence may range 
from zero to seven degrees. 
A grand trine between malefic planets, or benefic planets in their 
detriment, may also signal a difficult or unfavorable matter that is 
accomplished with ease. 
Horizon 
The horizon is also called the Ascendant-Descendant axis. It divides the 
horoscope into upper and lower sections. The upper portion is southern 
in direction and diurnal in time; the lower portion is northern in direc- 
tion and nocturnal in time.

Malefic 
A planet having an unfavorable influence. In medieval astrology, Mars 
and Saturn were considered malefic planets, which indicated difficulty, 
hardship, and loss. However, any planet can have a malefic or negative 
influence if poorly placed in a horoscope. See the discussion in this 
Appendix on Dignity. 
In a diurnal or day chart, Mars is more malefic in nature than Sat- 
urn. In a nocturnal or night chart, Saturn is more malefic than Mars. 
The position and placement of these planets will also describe the qual- 
ity or nature of these planets and how they operate in the horoscope. A 
well-placed Saturn is generally better than a poorly placed Venus. See 
also Benefic. 
Midheaven 
The Midheaven is the cusp of the tenth house, and is also called the 
MC, for "medium coeli." It is the most elevated position in a horoscope; 
see also Elevated/elevation. Its opposite fourth-house cusp is called the 
IC, or "imum coeli." 
Mutable signs 
Refers to the quality of the signs Gemini, Virgo, Sagittarius, and Pisces, 
which are generally characterized as flexible, adaptable, and changeable. 
Mutual reception 
This occurs when two planets occupy each other's sign of rulership, 
showing a relationship or willingness for one to assist the other in the 
areas that they rule. 
For example, Mercury in Libra and Saturn in Virgo are in mutual 
reception, and so help each other in matters involving analytical work, 
writing, or communication. 
Mutual reception can also occur by exaltation; Mars in Pisces and 
Venus in Capricorn is an example of mutual reception by exaltation. 
When two planets are in reception by rulership and exaltation, this is 
called mixed mutual reception: Saturn in Leo is in mixed mutual 

===== PAGE 157 =====

reception with the sun in Libra, because Saturn occupies the sign ruled 
by the sun, while the sun occupies the sign in which Saturn is exalted. 
Planets 
Planets are the celestial bodies in our solar system whose light is re- 
flected, not self-generated like stars. That is why planets do not twinkle 
like the stars do. 
The planets of our solar system are Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, 
Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune, and Pluto. The sun and moon are 
not planets but are collectively termed as such in this work for ease of 
reference. The sun and moon are more accurately referred to as the 
"luminaries" or "lights." 
Traditional or classical astrology does not recognize the outer planets 
Uranus, Neptune, and Pluto, because those planets were not known to 
early astrologers. Modern astrology makes use of these outer planets 
and assigns rulerships to them: Uranus is said to rule Aquarius, Nep- 
tune is said to rule Pisces, and Pluto is said to rule Scorpio. 
In my practice, I utilize the outer planets as descriptors but do not 
consider them as rulers of the zodiac signs. The outer planets generally 
have a malefic influence in forensic astrology. 
Progressions 
A progression is based on the movement of a planet from the time of 
birth, or from a specific given time, to another time. Progressions are 
used largely in predictive astrology and also to determine energies 
present at certain points during the lifetime. 
For example, in the most common form of progressions used, sec- 
ondary progressions, the sun progresses one degree for each year of the 
life. For example, if you were born with your sun at five degrees Leo, at 
age thirty, your sun will have progressed to five degrees of Virgo. 
Quality 
The qualities of the planets are categorized as follows: cardinal, fixed, 
and mutable. There are four signs belonging to each astrological qual- 
ity. 

===== PAGE 158 =====

Cardinal signs—Aries, Cancer, Libra, Capricorn 
Fixed signs—Taurus, Leo, Scorpio, Aquarius 
Mutable signs—Gemini, Virgo, Sagittarius, Pisces 
See also Cardinal, Fixed, Mutable. 
Rectification 
Astrologers use a system of calculations based on important events in 
the life to determine the exact time of a person's birth; this process is 
called rectification of a chart. 
For example, using the date and time of a person's wedding, an as- 
trologer can rectify a person's birth chart because certain planetary con- 
figurations are in evidence when a person marries, which can yield the 
information needed to cast the birth chart. This is a skilled process that 
requires expertise on the part of the astrologer. 
Retrograde 
This phenomenon occurs when a planet appears to be traveling back- 
ward in its motion from the point of view of the earth. The planet is not 
actually moving backward but only appears to do so from here. 
Planets in retrograde are considered to be weaker than when they are 
perceived to be moving in direct motion, and matters they represent 
often experience delays and setbacks during the retrograde period. Mer- 
cury goes retrograde the most frequently, about three times each year, 
while the other planets also have retrograde periods at less frequent 
intervals. The sun and moon never enter into retrograde motion. 
Rising 
A planet on or near the eastern horizon of the chart, or Ascendant. For 
example, Venus is in Aries at fourteen degrees, while the Ascendant is 
sixteen degrees Aries; Venus is rising. 
Ruler 
Each zodiac sign has a planetary ruler, and each planet in traditional as- 
trology rules two signs; the sun and moon, which are not planets in the 
true sense, each rule one sign: 

===== PAGE 159 =====

The sun rules Leo 
The moon rules Cancer 
Mercury rules Gemini and Virgo 
Venus rules Taurus and Libra 
Mars rules Aries and Scorpio 
Jupiter rules Sagittarius and Pisces 
Saturn rules Capricorn and Aquarius 
In traditional astrology, the outer planets—Uranus, Neptune, and 
Pluto—are not planetary rulers. 
Separating aspect 
A separating aspect has already completed its connection to another 
planet. For example, Venus at twenty-seven Sagittarius separates from a 
square with Saturn at twenty-five Virgo. 
Stellium 
A grouping of three or more planets in the same sign or house. For 
example, Neptune, Mercury, Venus, and the sun all in the sign Libra 
form a stellium in Libra. 
T square 
When two planets that are in opposition form a square to a third planet, 
forming a T-shaped configuration in the chart. For example, the moon 
at two Leo opposes Mars at five Aquarius. Both are in square aspect to 
Saturn at two Scorpio, forming a cross or T square. 
Tight aspect 
An aspect that is very nearly exact, within one degree on either side. 
Transit 
The passage of a planet in aspect to another planet or a specific point in 
a horoscope. For example, last year, Saturn, which was transiting the 
sign of Libra, made a conjunction by transit with my natal sun, which 
is in Libra in my birth horoscope. Transits are often one of the bases 
upon which predictions are made, although other factors also need to 

===== PAGE 160 =====

be taken into account. 
Trine 
A 120-degree relationship between two planets belonging to the same 
element. For example, the moon at twelve degrees Capricorn and Venus 
at thirteen degrees Taurus are in trine relationship to each other. The 
orb of influence may range from zero to seven degrees. 
Turning the chart 
This is a technique which is used to derive information from the hous- 
es in the chart in relation to a particular matter. We take the house 
representing the thing or person of interest, and, using it as the Ascen- 
dant, count off to the house governing the matter inquired about. 
For example, I am interested in my friend's child. My friend is ruled by 
the eleventh house. Children in general are ruled by the fifth house, 
but the children of my friend would be shown by the fi fth house count- 
ed from the eleventh. 
I can turn the chart to set the eleventh house as the Ascendant and then 
go to the fifth, or I can simply count five houses from the eleventh (in- 
cluding the eleventh), which brings me to the third house - the fifth 
from the eleventh. 
My father is ruled by the fourth house, money is ruled by the second, 
so I find my father's money by going to the second from the fourth, or, 
the fifth house. 
Void of course 
This term is commonly applied to the moon's motion. When the moon 
is passing through a sign without making aspects to the planets it is 
said to be void of course. Once it reaches the next sign it is no longer 
void of course. 
The void of course moon is important in horary astrology, where it may 
denote no action in a matter. In electional astrology it is not desirable to 
plan to begin a new matter under a void of course moon. 

===== PAGE 161 =====

In crime astrology I have found that a void of course planet, when 
representing a missing person or crime victim, may denote that the 
individual is no longer in life, or able to control his/her destiny. 
Water signs 
The water signs are Cancer, Scorpio, and Pisces, which constitute the 
watery triplicity. 
Zodiac 
The zodiac is the division of twelve sections or houses of a horoscope 
chart that relate to several different aspects and experiences of life. 
There are 360 degrees in a horoscope, and traditionally, the houses 
measured 30 degrees each. 
There are many different systems that determine the boundaries or 
cusps of each house: Placidus, Regiomontanus, Porphyry, Equal 
House, and Whole House systems, to name a few.

6. CRITICAL DEGREES 
The following degrees are considered significant in interpreting horo- 
scopes of events and are called "critical degrees": 
Four and seventeen degrees of mutable signs Gemini, Virgo, Sagit- 
tarius, Pisces 
Twelve and twenty-six degrees of cardinal signs Aries, Cancer, Libra, 
Capricorn 
Eight, nine, twenty-one, and twenty-two degrees of fixed signs Tau- 
rus, Leo, Scorpio, Aquarius 
Twenty-nine degrees of all signs. The late degrees of all signs should 
also be noted because they fall in tire terms of Mars or Saturn, tradi- 
tionally considered malefic in effect. Zero degrees of the cardinal signs 
are called the "Aries point," which in modern astrology relates to a drive 
for publicity. 
Twenty-two degrees of all signs; twenty-two degrees of Virgo houses 
the fixed star Denebola, which is prominent in shipwrecks and disas- 
ters at sea. This star is prominent in the forensic charts of the sinking 
of the Titanic and the Costa Concordia. 
Fifteen degrees of Leo is considered a degree of difficulty. 
A planet is within one degree of a fixed star is a critical placement. If 
the planet is Mars or Saturn, the effect is usually detrimental. Fixed 
stars and their meanings have been discussed throughout this book 
and the reader is referred to the works in the bibliography by Ebertin, 
Hoffman, Robson, and Brady.
