Fundamentals of Astrology Simply stated, astrology deals with the relationship between humankind and celestial phenomena. It is concerned with correlating planetary movements with the human condition and events on Earth. Various specialized branches of astrology are directed toward the study of human relationships, politics, current events, weather and the answers to specific questions of immediate interest. This book is devoted primarily to the branch called natal astrology, the study of one's personality and the conditions of one's life in all its facets according to the principles of astrology. A natal horoscope is a map of the heavens that charts the positions of the Sun, Moon and the eight planets (Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Satum, Uranus, Neptune and Pluto) as they appeared at the birthplace of a particular individual at the exact time of his or her birth. From this horoscope, sometimes called a birth map or nativity, deductions are made according to astrological logic. These deductions describe one's personal characteristics, behavior patterns, emotional responses, inborn drives, natural abilities, potential for achievement and capacity for growth and development as seen from the astrological point of view. Circumstances that arise in various areas of life are revealed in the horoscope. The apparent path of the Sun during the course of a year as viewed from Earth forms the basis for astrological measurements necessary to charting a horoscope. (A horoscope that shows the Earth at the center of the map and the positions of planets as observed from the birthplace is called a geocentric or Earth-centered map. Maps that chart the planets as observed from the Sun are heliocentric or Sun-centered maps.) To earthlings, the Sun appears to travel around Earth; in reality. Earth orbits the Sun, as do all planets in our solar system. The Tropical (movable) Zodiac follows the apparent path of the Sun. This path is called the ecliptic. The plane of the ecliptic is actually the plane in which Earth orbits the Sun. The planes of all planetary orbits except that of Pluto lie within 7° (seven degrees) of the plane of the eclipitc; Pluto's orbit varies about 17° from the ecliptic. The path of the ecliptic as it encircles the Earth forms an elliptical (oval) pattern that approximates a circle, which contains 360°. That part of space surrounding Earth, which includes the ecliptic and the orbits of the planets, forms the Zodiac Belt, a circular band approximately 18° thick that extends about 9° on either side of the ecliptic. This circular belt is divided into twelve equal sections of 30° each, designated signs of the zodiac. Signs of the zodiac describe parts of the heavens much like lines of longitude describe parts of Earth. The zodiacal position of a planet is called its zodiacal longitude. A circle such as the Zodiac Belt has no beginning or end, but for astrological purposes that point on the ecliptic where the Sun appears to cross over the equator as it travels from south to north each year is defined as 0° Aries. At that time, the time of the vernal equinox, the Sun appears to be directly above Earth's equator. The vernal equinox heralds the beginning of spring and the Sun s entry into the sign of Aries. The Sun's position on the ecliptic at the first moment of spring marks the starting point of the zodiac.
