### AQuari Pisces Cancer

Figure 1. The Zodiac Belt The signs of the Tropical Zodiac are not to be confused with constellations of the same name. At one time, over two thousand years ago, constellations and like signs coincided in space. However, at the spring and fall equinoxes the Sun appears to cross the equator slightly westward of the points it crossed the year before. Because of this gradual westward movement of the equinoctial points along the ecliptic, called Precession of the Equinoxes, the position of the Tropical Zodiac shifts slightly each year. In 1980 the constellations lagged about 24° behind signs of the same name. The constellation Pisces appeared in the background over the eastern horizon when the sign Aries rose over the horizon. The sidereal zodiac describes positions of constellations. The point 6° Pisces on the sidereal zodiac corresponds roughly to 0° Aries on the Tropical Zodiac. The word zodiac in this book refers to the Tropical Zodiac unless otherwise stated. The first coordinate for identifying a planet's position in space is zodiacal longitude. A second coordinate, declination, measures the distance in degrees a planet lies north or south of the celestial equator (the projection of Earth's equator into space). Zodiacal longitude and declination are the only measurements needed to pinpoint the location of a planet in our solar system. (Celestial latitude defines a planet's position north or south of the ecliptic. This text follows the more common practice of using declination instead.) Once a planet's zodiacal position is established, the planet is placed in the corresponding degree in the horoscope. The sign and degree of the zodiac that rises over the eastern horizon at the time of birth is the "starting point" or Ascendant of the horoscope. The Ascendant is marked in the eastern section of the horoscope; the remaining degrees of the zodiac continue counterclockwise around the horoscope in order until the circle is closed and the entire zodiac is contained within. The declination of a planet is not entered in the face of a horoscope; it is listed separately. Beginning with the Ascendant, the horoscope is sectioned into twelve parts called houses, each numbered consecutively from one to twelve in the same order as the signs, counterclockwise. The point of the zodiac at which a house begins is the house cusp. A house ends where the next begins. Cusp lines which separate the houses resemble the spokes of a wheel (see Figure 2). After the houses are marked in a horoscope, the planets are entered in the houses according to sign and degree. The planets, signs and houses have specific functions in a horoscope, functions that are interrelated. Each house represents a particular area of life; the planets activate the affairs of the houses and energize the drives of the individual; the signs influence the expression of planetary drives and house affairs. The houses are divisions of a horoscope. The signs are divisions of the zodiac. Note that the directional points east, west, north and south in an astrological map are the reverse of those designated on a geographic map (see Figure 2). Certain of the planets, signs and houses are closely allied by virtue of natural rulership. Each house is associated with a particular sign called its natural sign; each sign is ruled by a particular planet(s) called its natural planetary ruler; each house is ruled (governed) naturally by the planet(s) that rules its natural sign.
