### Planetary Motion

The planets travel at varying rates of speed as they follow their orbits around the Sun. Because a planetary orbit is elliptical, some points of the orbital path lie closer to the Sun than others. The nearer to the Sun a planet moves, the faster it travels along its orbit. The point of closest approach to the Sun is the perihelion of orbit; the farthest point is the aphelion. The Moon's joumey through the zodiac depends upon its orbit of Earth. The point in the lunar orbit nearest the Earth is the perigee; the most distant point is the apogee. Distant planets take longer to complete their orbits and, therefore, longer to traverse the zodiac. The planets move through the zodiac in order of fastest moving to slowest as follows: The Moon, Mercury, Venus, the Sun, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune and Pluto. The time it takes a planet to orbit the Sun is called the period of orbit; the time a planet spends completing one circuit of the zodiac is the zodiacal period. The average longitudinal distance (distance in arc) a planet moves each day is the mean daily motion. When a planet moves faster than the mean daily motion it is said to be in swift motion; when it moves more slowly than the mean, it is said to be slow of course. Planetary motion is described as direct (D), retrograde (R) or stationary (S). Actually, all planetary motion is forward or direct. Relative motion is not. For example, when sitting in a parked car have you suddenly hit the brake because your car started to drift backward only to realize that your car had not moved? The car beside yours had pulled ahead. You may experience a similar sensation when riding on a train. If parallel traffic moves faster than the train, you feel like you are moving in reverse, providing there are no landmarks or changing scenery to help you get your bearings. If the train is moving at the exact speed and in the same direction as traffic, you seem to be motionless or stationary. You are not passing the traffic nor is it pulling ahead of you. In other words, the relative motion between the train and the traffic is zero. Once one or the other speeds up, slows down or alters direction, there is a change in relative motion. If a fast-moving car is assumed to be a stationary reference point, a more slowly moving train is traveling backward from the car's point of view. From the train's point of view, the car is moving ahead. Planetary motion is much more complicated than that of either a car or a train. Celestial measurements depend upon the frame of reference of the observer and the angle of observation. For astrological measurements, Earth is the designated frame of reference; as observed from Earth, another planet may appear to be in retrograde motion (moving backward in the zodiac), stationary (motionless, stopped), or in direct motion (moving forward in the zodiac). The only time a planet is stationary is during the transition from direct motion to retrograde or vice versa. A planet is said to "make a station" at the degree of the zodiac where it apparently stops. A planet has greater influence in the horoscope when it is stationary because its energy is concentrated

### Asteroids

In late 1977, astronomer Charles Kowal discovered a heavenly body that appears to have some of the physical characteristics of a planet but is within the size range of an asteroid, being only about four hundred miles in diameter; however, its orbit falls in the area of the outer planets, not in the Asteroid Belt. For the time being, this body has been dubbed a planetoid and named Chiron. It will be interesting to see what astrological influence, if any, study of this tiny planet-like body will reveal in the future. Study of four other asteroids, which were discovered in the early nineteenth century, has been stepped up since 1973. These asteroids are Ceres, Pallas (Pallas Athena), Juno and Vesta. There is no general agreement as to th? astrological significance or rulerships attached to these asteroids. Many consider the zodiacal position of an asteroid at birth to be a sensitive point in the horoscope that describes general conditions that emerge later in life when planets progress to or aspect its position. Evidence is as yet too scanty to provide an accurate evaluation. In 1980, the asteroids were not generally accepted as basic elements of natal astrology.

### Planet Classifications

Planets are classified benefic or malefic according to the ease with which planetary energy may be utilized constructively under difficult astrological circumstances. These terms are somewhat misleading; planetary energy is neither "good" (benefic) nor "bad" (malefic) per se. Like sign traits, planetary qualities can manifest in a positive (constructive) or negative (destructive) manner. However, improper use of the energies of benefics is potentially less damaging than misuse of the energies of malefics. Jupiter is the Greater Benefic; Venus, the Lesser Benefic. The Sun, Moon and Mercury are moderately benefic. Satum is the Greater Malefic; Mars, the Lesser Malefic. Uranus, Neptune and Pluto complete the list of malefics. Planets are also classed as masculine or feminine. Mercury, which is androgynous (neutral), assumes the gender of the sign in which it is placed. Planets are described as hot, warm, cold, dry, moist, electric, magnetic, fruitful and/or barren.
