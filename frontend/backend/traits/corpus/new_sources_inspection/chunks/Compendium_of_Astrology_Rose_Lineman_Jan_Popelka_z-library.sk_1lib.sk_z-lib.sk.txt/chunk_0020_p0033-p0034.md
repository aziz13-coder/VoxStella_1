### Planetary Standing

The strength of a planet in a horoscope depends upon sign position, horoscope placement, aspects and motion. Each planet rules one or two signs. A planet that occupies a sign it rules is dignified. A dignified planet is strengthened, and its energy is manifested in pure form. Aside from that of dignity, one other sign, called

A planet is essentially dignified when it falls in a sign of dignity or exaltation. Planets that occupy a sign of essential dignity command added strength in the horoscope. When a planet occupies the sign opposite that of dignity, it is in the sign of detriment: when a planet is in the sign opposite that of exaltation, it is in fall. Planetary action is weakened when a planet is in its sign of detriment or fall; then it is considered to be debilitated. Mutual reception strengthens planetary principles even if a planet is in a sign of debilitation. Mutual reception occurs when two planets are each placed in the other's sign of essential dignity. For example, if the Sun is in Cancer or Taurus, the Moon's signs of rulership and exaltation, respectively, at the same time the Moon is in Leo of Aries, the Sun's signs of essential dignity (the Sun rules Leo and is exalted in Aries), these two planets are in mutual reception. Planets in mutual reception support each other; they are deemed as effective as if each were placed in its own sign of essential dignity. If a planet occupies a sign other than one of its essential dignity or debilitation, and it is not in mutual reception with another planet, it is said to be peregrine. A peregrine planet lacks standing: its dynamic expression may be inhibited or reduced depending upon other elements in the horoscope. Planetary strength cannot be determined on the basis of one criterion; planetary standing in the horoscope depends upon all relevant factors. A specific degree of the zodiac, called the degree of exaltation, is assigned to each planet, A planet functions freely in the degree of exaltation. At the opposite point of the zodiac, the degree of fall, natural expression of planetary energy is constrained. A planet is accidentally dignified and thereby strengthened when it is placed in its natural house in the horoscope or within five degrees of one of the four angles of the horoscope. The angles are: (1) the Ascendant and (2) its opposite point, the Descendant, (3) the Medium Coeli, commonly called the Midheaven and abbreviated MC, which is the highest point or culminating degree of the ecliptic, and (4) the point opposite the MC, the Imum Coeli, customarily shortened to 1C. The MC and IC are mistakenly referred to as the zenith and nadir respectively; the zenith and nadir are the poles of the horizon not the ecliptic. The closer to the MC a planet falls, the more powerful it is in the horoscope. Planets that occupy the southern hemisphere of the horoscope (above the horizontal axis) are more influential than those in the northern hemisphere (lower half of the horoscope) except for the Moon whose natural position is in the night section below the horizontal axis. The Moon functions equally well above or below the horizon. Planets that are placed in a house of natural rulership or in a house they rule in the horoscope are given greater emphasis regardless of the hemisphere in which they are located. A planet's effectiveness in the horoscope depends in part upon the number and type of aspects it forms with other planets. Through aspects, the energies of two planets may unite and support mutual action; energies may combine to nullify or weaken the action of one or both planets involved; the action of one planet may obstruct and frustrate the action of another in aspect. Energies may oppose or they may blend harmoniously, depending upon the type and strength of the aspect and the nature of the planets involved, A planet is said to be well aspected if it forms harmonious aspects with other planets; if it forms disharmonious aspects, it is said to be adversely aspected or afflicted. Planets that form a large number of harmonious aspects are more powerful than those which form few aspects or are afflicted. The energies of well-aspected planets flow smoothly and naturally. The energy of an afflicted planet is in some way distorted or weakened. A planet that forms no aspects has no channel through which to direct its energy; its influence in the horoscope is limited. Planets moving in direct motion are emphasized over those in retrograde motion; stationary planets supercede both in terms of planetary standing, other factors being equal. One of the most important planets in a horoscope is the planet that rules the sign on the Ascendant. This planet is the horoscope ruler (chart ruler). If this planet occupies a sign of detriment and or torms few aspects, or is otherwise weakened, its importance is not lessened, although it lacks strength in the horoscope. A weak or afflicted horoscope ruler makes personal expression more difficult since this planet is overshadowed by other energies. This in itself is significant.
