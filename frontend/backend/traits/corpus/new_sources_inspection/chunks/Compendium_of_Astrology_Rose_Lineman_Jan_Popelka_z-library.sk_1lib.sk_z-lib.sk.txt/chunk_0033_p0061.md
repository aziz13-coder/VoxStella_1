### The Vertex-Anti-Vertex Axis

The Vertex marks the intersection of the ecliptic and the prime vertical (the vertical great circle that passes through the east point of Earth's horizon, zenith and nadir) in the west; the opposite point is the Anti- Vertex. The axis connecting these two points is called the third angle of the horoscope. The astrological significance of these two points and the hemispheric division created by their axis is not fully established. It is believed that the Vertex indicates fateful or karmic events that play an important part in the life of an individual. Vertex events are not fateful in the sense that something must happen; these events are related to cause and effect. For example, a person through his or her actions (Anti-Vertex) can set up chain reactions which lead to a specific result (Vertex). The Anti- Vertex indicates self-action that begins the chain and the Vertex indicates the point of result. If, however, action is changed or modified anywhere along the line, the end result will be changed or modified accordingly. The Vertex is also known as the wish-fulfillment factor, but wishes are apt to be fulfilled in a very perverse manner. For instance, if the Vertex activates the eighth house, which rules insurance and legacies, the wish for a large sum of money might be fulfilled through collection of insurance stemming from destruction of possessions or through a legacy, a direct result of a death. Either way, suffering, tragedy and/or sacrifice is the price of wish fulfillment. Self-activation (Anti-Vertex) in the form of the wish involved second house (financial) matters in this case, perhaps because of unconscious desires for economic security or financial status. The Vertex is always on the western side of a horoscope and usually falls^between the fifth and eighth houses. As a rule, other people are involved in whatever is implied by the sign, house, and aspects associated with this point. Personal relationships may be significant here, or others may be responsible for related external conditions, depending upon all astrological factors concerned. The Anti-Vertex, found in the eastern section of the chart, indicates the point of beginning action in relation to the Vertex. Much more needs to be learned about the true nature of the Vertex in order to determine its exact significance in natal astrology. Current data does suggest that a conjunction between the Vertex and a planet (both in the same sign and degree) lends power to both. For example, if Neptune conjuncts the Vertex in the seventh house, which rules marriage and partnerships, unrealistic (Neptune) expectancies (Vertex) in matters concerning partners and mate (seventh house) are indicated. Expectations are selforiented (Anti-Vertex in the first house opposite the seventh house); social interaction (seventh house) depends upon personal development, a first house matter. If the conjunction is in Virgo, the individual might expect perfection in relationships and be disappointed if hoped-for perfection failed to materialize. However, the ability to handle these relationships comes through self-development and self-understanding as indicated by the Anti-Vertex. Other planets that influence the conjunction would indicate how the individual might deal with Vertexrelated situations. It is good practice to insert the Vertex, abbreviated V, in a horoscope and study its connection with actual events or relationships that seem to be related. Calculations for computing the zodiacal positions of the Vertex and Anti-Vertex are given in Chapter 12.

### East Point

The East Point is the precise degree of zodiacal longitude rising at the equator at the time of birth. Also called the equatorial Ascendant or hidden Ascendant, the East Point describes the inner or hidden side of the personality. These traits, though not always observable, frequently manifest when the individual is under stress. Aspects to the East Point give important clues as to how one forms and maintains relationships.
