A planet that occupies its sign of dignity may serve as indirect dispositor for several planets. It is possible for one planet to act as dispositor or indirect dispositor for the remaining nine planets. When this happens the dispositing planet becomes the final dispositor of the horoscope and, as such, bears influence on the overall tenor of the horoscope. Consider the planetary arrangement shown in Figure 8: Sun in Leo, Moon in Leo, Mercury in Leo, Venus in Cancer, Mars in Gemini, Jupiter in Leo, Saturn in Virgo, Uranus in Scorpio, Neptune in Sagittarius and Pluto in Libra. The Sun disposits the Moon, Mercury and Jupiter because these planets are placed in Leo, and the Sun rules Leo. The Sun in its sign of natural rulership has no dispositor. Venus is disposited by the Moon, ruler of Cancer; in turn, the Moon is disposited by the Sun, which therefore indirectly disposits Venus. Mars in Gemini and Saturn in Virgo are both disposited by Mercury, ruler of Gemini and Virgo. The Sun indirectly disposits Mars and Saturn because it disposits Mercury. Uranus is disposited by Mars and Pluto, rulers of Scorpio; Mars is indirectly disposited by the Sun and Pluto is disposited by Venus, which is indirectly disposited by the Sun; therefore, both Pluto and Uranus are indirectly disposited by the Sun. Neptune in Sagittarius is disposited by Jupiter, which is also disposited by the Sun. Thus, all planets lead to the Sun; the chain stops with the Sun because the Sun acts as its own dispositor. In this example, the Sun is the final dispositor. During days in which no planet changes sign, the final dispositor, if one exists, is the same in the horoscope of every person bom that day. Because of astrological variations observed at different times and in different localities, a single planet seldom influences any two horoscopes in an identical manner. The final dispositor links all planets in a horoscope and thereby colors all planetary action; it describes underlying motivation that prompts expression of planetary drives, motivation contingent upon the nature of the dispositor and its bearing in the horoscope. The basic principles of a final dispositor describe general motivation. Its influence is broad and touches all areas of life. The Sun as final dispositor indicates that the need for ego-satisfaction and self-esteem is linked to other drives. For example, Venus rules the principle of attraction, but the desire to attract others is a matter of ego if the Sun is final dispositor. Persons whose Sun is the final dispositor seek relationships and activities that enhance self-esteem. The Moon as final dispositor shows that emotional needs and family^ considerations underlie decisions and actions. These people enter jobs and form relationships that provide emotional security and inner satisfaction. Mercury as final dispositor indicates intellectual motivation. These people base decisions on logic; they choose companions and activities that provide mental stimulation. If Mercury is in Virgo rather than Gemini, the drive for perfection is evident as well. In Taurus, Venus as final dispositor emphasizes material needs. The desire for financial well-being and physical comfort underlies all decisions and actions. Venus in Libra as final dispositor brings out needs for companionship and harmony. Decisions and activities reflect a desire for balance and cooperation. Mars in Aries as final dispositor motivates physical action on all levels. These people find no satisfaction in sedentary interests. They act decisively to keep their affairs moving. In Scorpio, Mars as final dispositor links sexual needs to other drives. Sexual energies stimulate creativity, worldly ambition, sense of purpose and activity in all affairs. But fulfillment is incomplete if sexual satisfaciton, a prime motivating factor, is lacking. Jupiter as final dispositor stirs motivation based on the need for truth and integrity in actions and relationships. Decisions spring from unselfish desires. A strong element of self-sacrifice, not shown with Jupiter in Sagittarius, is pronounced if the planet is in Pisces. Jupiter in Sagittarius inspires intellectual motivation; in Pisces, emotional motivation. With Saturn as final dispositor in Capricorn, worldly ambition is the driving force behind planetary drives. The need for recognition and status colors decisions and actions in personal affairs as well as in mundane pursuits. Motivation is essentially selfish; in Aquarius, Saturn-inspired selfishness is tempered by humane considerations, but ambitious drive remains the prime motivating factor. Uranus as final dispositor describes motivation rooted in the need for change. If Uranus is well aspected, it is a most significant indication of progress.

If Pluto is heavily afflicted, corruption can be a product of power drives. Two planets in mutual reception share final dispositorship if they jointly serve as dispositors or indirect dispositors for the remaining eight planets. If one of the joint dispositors has greater standing in a horoscope, the stronger planet is the more influential of the two in respect to dispositorship. If Mars in Capricorn (Saturn's sign of dignity) shares dispositorship with Saturn in Aries (Mars' sign of dignity) by virtue of mutual reception. Mars is the more influential dispositor of the two because Mars is exalted in Capricorn and Saturn is in fall in Aries. A mutual reception between the Moon in Sagittarius and Jupiter in Cancer shows Jupiter to be the stronger dispositor because Jupiter is exalted in Cancer and the Moon is peregrine (lacks standing) in Sagittarius. If two planets in mutual reception have equal standing in the horoscope according to sign, house placement and aspects, they exert equal influence as joint dispositors. There can be no final dispositor in a horoscope unless at least one planet occupies a sign it rules or two planets are in mutual reception. If more than one planet is dignified and each disposits one or more of the remaining planets, the dispositing planets are given added weight in the horoscope, although influence is not as widespread as that of a final dispositor. With no planets in ruling signs, it is possible to have a continuous circle of dispositors. The Shaping of the Horoscope Though many horoscopes show similarities, few are exactly alike. The disparity of a few miles in birth location alters the horoscopes of persons bom at the same time. In the natal charts of twins, the difference of a few minutes in birthtime results in slight but often important variations between the two horoscopes. Nevertheless, many horoscopes show similar planetary arrangements. The distribution of the planets around the horoscope forms recognizable planetary patterns or pictures. Seven such patterns have been identified by the eminent astrologer. Marc Edmund Jones {The Guide to Horoscope Interpretation, Philadelphia, Pennsylvania; David McKay, 1941), A horoscope is classified according to its shaping, that is, according to the pattern that the planets form within the horoscope. The planetary pattern has a bearing on the general character of the horoscope. Shaping is considered part of the overall picture. It does not overshadow or equal specific astrological factors such as planetary influence or aspects, although it may reinforce these things. The extent and direction of shaping emphasis is determined by the total horoscope. All horoscopes fall into one of the seven categories described by Jones.
