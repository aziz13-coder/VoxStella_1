### The Splash

The Splash is indicated by a fairly uniform planetary arrangement. The planets are more or less evenly distributed around the horoscope. Usually no more than two planets occupy any one sign. The space between planets is seldom greater than that covered by two consecutive signs or two consecutive houses. Ideally, planets should be placed no more than a sextile (60°) apart. This scattering of planets suggests a scattering or dispersion of interests. Splash people are versatile. They involve themselves in a wide range of activities. Because of such diversity, interest may be superficial if the rest of the horoscope shows lack of depth or perseverance.

### The Bucket

The Bucket is similar to the Bowl. In this configuration nine planets occupy onehalf of the horoscope. One planet is placed in the other half, roughly opposing the group of nine, forming the "handle" of the bucket. If the handle planet is the only planet occupying that particular hemisphere, it is called a singleton. Two planets placed in close conjunction, within a degree of each other, may serve together as a bucket handle for the eight planets occupying the opposite hemisphere. Bucket people bear some similarity to Bowl people in that activity is directed toward the occupied section of the horoscope. However, the handle planet is the focal point of action. A heavy planet at the handle gives balance to the horoscope; a lighter planet such as the Moon is unable to focus planetary energies as effectively as a heavier one unless it is in close conjunction with another planet, then the two planets function together.

### The Bundle

The Bundle consists of a tightly packed planetary arrangement. All ten planets are confined to the space of a trine (120°) or very nearly so. Only four or possibly five consecutive signs are occupied by planets. About two-thirds of the horoscope is empty. The Bundle is uncommon and so is the individual in whose horoscope it appears. Bundle people are in some way unique. They confine and focus their interests, specializing in the area(s) indicated by the most heavily occupied part of the horoscope. These people are self-contained and narrow. Because planetary energy is concentrated in such a small portion of the chart, the scope of experience is somewhat limited, but activity in that area is powerful. The planet leading clockwise directs planetary drives.

### The Locomotive

^ The Locomotive is the pictorial opposite of the Bundle. All ten planets are placed within the space of two consecutive trines (240°); one trine (120°) is empty. This planetary arrangement occupies twothirds of the horoscope; one-third is devoid of planets. Locomotive people are self-motivated and self-driven. Essentially goal-oriented, they are dynamic people who seem to possess almost unlimited energy. Once they achieve a particular objective, they are ready to begin working toward another. The planet leading clockwise signifies motivation; the house it occupies shows the main focus of energy.

### The Splay

The Splay is somewhat difficult to identify. It has been referred to as the "catchall"—the shaping used when nothing else fits. This is not correct. The Splay consists of an irregular planetary distribution that ^ contains two or more clusters of planets bunched together at random points in the horoscope, A lack of symmetry and irregularly spaced planet clusters identify the Splay. Splay people are individualists who resist the system. They refuse to be regimented or limited by anything or anyone but themselves. They have their own set of rules, unique tastes and self-directed interests. Intense and purposeful, they direct their energies in accordance with planetary placement in the horoscope. Few horoscopes meet the ideal requirements of a shaping category. It is recommended that the student read Jones' book, The Guide to Horoscope Interpretation, and study the numerous examples and variations he illustrates.

10 The Lunation Cycle and Eclipses The changing face of the Moon has fascinated earthlings since time immemorial. Who has not watched the almost invisible new Moon "grow" into a round full Moon and then "shrink" to another new Moon? As the Moon travels around Earth, following Earth's orbit of the Sun, it turns on its own axis. In so doing it seems to change shape. Of course, it does not, but as its position changes relative to Sun and Earth so does the visible portion of the Moon, the portion that reflects sunlight to Earth.
