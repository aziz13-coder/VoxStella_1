### The Lunation Cycle

The time interval between two successive new Moons (lunations), the lunation cycle, is about twenty-nine and one-half days. The lunation cycle is divided into two phases, waxing and waning (see Chapter 4). Each of these phases is sub-divided into two quarters; each of the four quarters consists of two stages. The first stage of the first quarter of the lunation cycle begins with the new Moon, starting the waxing phase. At the time of lunation, the Moon is in the same sign and degree of the zodiac as the Sun (conjunct the Sun). As the Moon moves away from the Sun's zodiacal position, a crescent becomes visible within a few days. The crescent Moon completes the first stage of the first quarter of the lunation cycle. In approximately one week following the new Moon, a waxing half-moon, called the first quarter Moon, appears, ending the first quarter and beginning the second. At this point in the lunation cycle, the Moon's zodiacal position is 90° past that of the Sun. The first quarter Moon enlarges to a gibbous Moon (about three-quarters of a circle) in the first stage of the second quarter, then swells to a full Moon to mark the completion of both the second quarter and the waxing phase and the beginning of the third quarter and the waning phase (see Figure 10). At the full Moon, the Sun and Moon lie exactly opposite each other in the zodiac; the Moon then starts to approach the zodiacal position of the Sun.

### depends upon the lunar cycle as follows

New Moon (Moon conjunct Sun to Moon 45° ahead of Sun): Motivation is instinctive, actions impulsive. People born during this period in the lunation cycle seek and meet challenges eagerly. They are magnetic and relate vividly to others. Because they project feeling and personality, many are good actors.

### Crescent Moon (Moon 45° to 90° ahead of the Sun)

This stage represents struggle against the established pattern; the new is pitted against the old, but the old always hangs on in some form. These people strive to break with the past and seek new vistas. First Quarter Moon (Moon 90° to 135° ahead of the Sun): These people are survivors; they act through and deal with crises. They attract active, stimulating, noisy situations. Dynamic and energetic (within sign dictates), they may try to move ahead too quickly. Gibbous Moon (Moon 135° to 180°—opposite—ahead of the Sun): Motivation is based in selfanalysis. These people are conscious of their actions and feel prompted to make life something of value. They tend to keep a running score or evaluation of personal progress.

### Full Moon (Moon opposite to 135° behind the Sun)

Motivation stems from relationships; action is objective, structured, purposeful. These people seek self-perfection through bonding instinct and intellect. Disseminating Moon (Moon 135° to 90° behind the Sun): Distribution of knowlege and information is important in this phase. They may be prophets of the future or martyrs to it. The rhythmic cycle of the Moon describes a fluid, changing relationship as the Moon moves through the signs. Two successive lunations usually occur in sequential signs of the zodiac but not always. Sometimes when a new Moon occurs in an early degree of a sign, the next new Moon falls in a late degree of the same sign. A new Moon that occurs in the same sign as the previous lunation signifies the need to re-evaluate relationships as indicated by sign and horoscope position. The Moon crosses the ecliptic an average of twice during a lunation cycle, once at its North Node while traveling from south to north, and once at its South Node as it moves southward (see the section on the Moon's Nodes in Chapter 3).
