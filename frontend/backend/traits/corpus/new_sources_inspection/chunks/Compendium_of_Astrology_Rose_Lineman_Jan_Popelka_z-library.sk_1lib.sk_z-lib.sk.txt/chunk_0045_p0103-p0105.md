11 The Took of Astrology Because numerous computer services are now available, many astrologers no longer construct horoscopes. These astrologers devote their skills to delineating (interpreting) horoscopes. For the person seriously interested in astrology, however, there are advantages to knowing how to correctly erect one. An astrologer who is familiar with the calculations can readily spot any errors that may exist in a prepared horoscope. As beginners learn how to calculate and erect an accurate horoscope, they gain understanding of the basis for astrology. One begins to get the feel of a horoscope during the process of drawing it up. The two major steps in calculating a horoscope are as follows: (1) determining the house cusps and (2) locating the planets. The essential data required are the exact time, date and place of birth. The following tools are needed: (1) an atlas or longitude and latitude table, (2) a time zone/time changes book, (3) a Table of Houses and (4) an ephemeris. Prepared chart forms (horoscope blanks), printed calculation sheets and a calculator are helpful Geographic Longitude and Latitude It is necessary to describe the location of the birthplace in terms consistent with those used in astrological calculations. The location of a place is defined by its geographic longitude and latitude. Longitude and latitude tables list the locations of numerous towns and cities throughout the world. Longitude and latitude are given in degrees and minutes. Some tables also include seconds of arc. The symbol for degrees is _L, for minutes: ' and for seconds: Jl. One writes 10° 35' 06" instead of 10 degrees, 35 minutes and 6 seconds. There are sixty seconds of arc in each minute of longitude and latitude and sixty minutes in each degree of longitude and latitude. One should not confuse minutes and seconds of longitude or latitude with minutes and seconds of time. Longitude describes the distance in arc a place is situated east or west of Greenwich, England. The longitude of Greenwich is 0°. In writing, the direction of longitude follows the degrees. West is abbreviated "W" and east "E." Eighty degrees west longitude is written: 80° W. If minutes and/or seconds are included, the direction of longitude is inserted between the degrees and minutes. Eighty degress and forty-eight minutes west longitude is written as 80° W 48'. Latitude describes the distance in arc a place is located north or south of the Earth's equator. The latitude at the equator is 0°. Latitude is written in the same style as longitude: "N" stands for north and "S" stands for south. Forty-three degrees and seventeen minutes south latitude is written 43° S 17'. Cities and towns are arranged alphabetically in longitude and latitude tables according to country and state, or province where applicable (Meridians of longitude pass through the North and South Poles as they encircle the Earth. The prime meridian, 0° longitude, from which all other meridian lines are measured passes through Greenwich. England. Lines of latitude are drawn parallel to the Earth's equator, which is designated 0° latitude.)

### Time

Time zone/time changes books give the world standard time zones and the Standard Time Meridian (longitude) on which a country or section of a country bases its clock time. There are twenty-four standard time zones in the world. Clock time increases one hour for each 15° longitude east of Greenwich and decreases one hour for each 15° longitude west of Greenwich. Planetary positions are listed in reference books according to Greenwich Mean Time (universal time), which is standard clock time in Greenwich, England. Birthtime must be converted to Greenwich Mean Time (GMT) in order to complete the calculations that are needed to erect an accurate horoscope. Standard time zones are designated by a number and/or a letter of the alphabet. The number assigned to a time zone indicates the number of hours by which standard clock time in that particular zone differs from Greenwich Mean Time. Greenwich is in the zero zone. A plus sign (+) preceding a time zone number signifies that the specified number of hours is added to standard clock time in that zone to equal Greenwich Mean Time. Time zone hours are added to standard clock time in the western hemisphere in order to convert to Greenwich Mean Time because clock time in places west of Greenwich decreases one hour for every 15° longitude. A minus sign (—) preceding a time zone number indicates that the hour(s) differential is subtracted from standard clock time in that zone to convert to Greenwich Mean Time. Time zone hours are subtracted from clock time in the eastern hemisphere to convert to Greenwich Mean Time because clock time increases one hour for each 15° longitude east of Greenwich. Letters of the alphabet are sometimes used to designate time zones. Zero degrees longitude, at which Greenwich lies, is the Standard Time Meridian for the "Z" zone; each 15° zone east of Greenwich is lettered consecutively "A" through "M" with the omission of zones west of Greenwich are lettered "N" through "Y" (refer to Figure 12).
