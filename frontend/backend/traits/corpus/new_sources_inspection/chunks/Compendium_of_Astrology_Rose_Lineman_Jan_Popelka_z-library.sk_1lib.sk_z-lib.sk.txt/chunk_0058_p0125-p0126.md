The log table in Figure 17 shows that the anti-log of 1.8573 is 20', It is not always necessary to calculate the time correction for a given house cusp at the next higher degree of latitude in order to determine the cusp distance required in the correction for latitude. In many instances a house cusp moves an equal distance between the same two latitudes at both the earlier and later sidereal times closest to the Local Sidereal Time of birth; if so, the time corrected cusp must move that same distance during the same latitude interval because the Local Sidereal Time falls between the two listed times. The correct cusp distance can then be found by calculating the difference between the positions listed for the house cusp at the two latitudes of interest at either the closest earlier sidereal time or the later sidereal time. Refer to Chapter 11, Figure 13. The positions of the intermediate house cusps are listed in degrees and tenths of a degree. Tenths of a degree are converted to minutes by multiplying the digit following the decimal point by 6. The number of degrees and the decimal point are not repeated for each row in the intermediate columns. If none appears in the row of interest, look up the column to the first degree and decimal point entry that precedes the row of interest.
