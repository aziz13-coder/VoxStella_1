### Unknoion Bhihtune

There are methods, based on actual events in a person's life, by which an unknown birthtimecan be ascertained (rectified). A solar chart, which places the first house cusp in the position of the Sun (conjunct the Sun) as listed in the ephemeris on the birthdate, is often used for persons whose birthtimes are uncertain and unrectified. The houses, called solar houses, follow as in an Equal Househoroscopewithcuspsignsappearing in natural sequence beginning with the Sun sign on the first house. Planetary positions are entered as given in the ephemeris (uncorrected). A solar chart is not equivalent to a horoscope erected for the time and location of birth. It has no Ascendant orMCandany reference to the Ascendant, MC or other angles is excluded. Otherwise, houses have the same meaning as those in a regular horoscope. Interpretation relates the individuaFs potentials to the Sun, which represents the self and the life force. Such a chart with its narrow perspectivegives a somewhat limited view based on the Sun s influence in the person's life. Some astrologers prefer to use a sunrise chart, cast for the time of sunrise on the day of birth at the birthplace, if the birthtime is uncertain. This type of chart can be drawn up either as an equal house or quadrant (Placidean, etc.) horoscope substituting the time of sunrise for birthtime in calculations. It depicts the astrological tone set at the beginning of the day, which has universal application and is, therefore, applicable to the individual in a general way. Another method for charting houses when the birthtime is unknown uses a natural (flat) chart which has 0° Aries on the cusp of the first house followed by natural houses and signs as shown in Chapter 1. Figure 2. This chart shows the relationship between planets as they appear on the birthdate (as listed in an ephemeris) and theirnatural signs and houses. Since each sign and planet has influence in its natural house in any horoscope, many astrologers believe that the flat chart more closely describes the individual s needs motivations and potentials than either a solar or sunrise chart does.

Locating the Vertex, Anti-Vertex and East Point To locate the Vertex (V), find in the Table of Houses the Ascendant listed for co-latitude (90° minus birth latitude) under the Midheaven, which equals the natal IC. The Anti-Vertex (A-V) is the opposite point. For example, the IC (cusp of the fourth house) for the horoscope shown in Chapter 11, Figure 14, erected for 41° north latitude, is 1° Leo. The co-latitude for this chart is 49° (90° — 41°), The Ascendant listed at 49° under the MC 1° Leo is 24o05' Libra, the Vertex; the Anti-Vertex is 24° 05' Aries. The co-latitude of Susan's birthplace is 48° 36' (refer to Example 2 for birth data); her IC is 1047' Leo. Interpolation shows that the Vertex for her chart is at 24047' Libra in the sixth house of her horoscope. The East Point (E or E. Pt.) is the Ascendant listed at 0° latitude for the Local Sidereal Time calculated for a birth, the ST used to find house cusp listings. If the Table of Houses does not include 0° latitude, the latitude at the equator, the position of the East Point can be calculated by adding 6 hours to the Local Sidereal Time of Birth and finding the MC for this advanced ST. The East Point at birth is at the same position as the MC listed for the advanced ST. The East Point is corrected in the same manner as the MC if the exact time of interest is not listed. For Susan's horoscope, the LST is 20:16:07 (Example 2); the advanced ST is 2:16:07 (20:16:07 + 6:00:00 = 26:16:07, reduced by 24 hours = 2:16:07). The MC given for the nearest earlier sidereal time is 6° Taurus; interpolation locates the exact East Point at 6° 21' Taurus, the same position as listed for 20:16:07, the LST, at 0° latitude. The Vertex and East Point may be entered in the horoscope or listed on the chart form.

Charting the Planets and Moon's Nodes The planetary positions entered in the ephemeris for a particular date are exact at the ephemeris reference time on that date. The listed positions must be adjusted to account for planetary motion during the time interval between the GMT birthtime and the GMT ephemeris reference time. The reference time for a noon ephemeris is 12:00 p.m. GMT: the reference time for a zero hour ephemeris is 00:00:00 GMT (12:00 a.m.).
