Entering the Planets and Nodes in the Chart Form Before the planets are entered in the horoscope, it is good practice to check the approximate position of the Sun in the horoscope against the birthtime. Think of the horoscope as a clock with each house representing a two-hour interval. Place 6:00 a.m. at the Ascendant, 12:00 noon at the MC, 6:00 p.m. at the Descendant and 12:00 midnight at the IC. The Ascendant is more correctly associated with sunrise, which may occur earlier or later than 6:00 a.m. However, if the Sun does not fall within a few degrees of the house that corresponds to the birthtime, calculations should be checked. The planets and the Moon's North Node are entered in the horoscope according to their corrected positions. The Moon's South Node may be omitted, but it is understood that the South Node occupies the point in the zodiac and the horoscope that lies directly opposite the position of the North Node. The planets and the signs they occupy are designated in the horoscope by the corresponding glyphs. Planetary glyphs (symbols) are entered in the face of the horoscope near the outer ring. The zodiacal position may be written from left to right or vertically, whichever is more legible. The notation for the zodiacal position may precede or follow the planetary glyph. All planetary entries (glyphs and position designations) are placed within the outer circle and written so they may be read from a normal reading position without turning the horoscope around. Styles vary, but it is common practice to insert the sign designation between the degrees and minutes of zodiacal longitude, as is displayed in Figure 18. The placement of the planets in the horoscope depends upon the house system and type of chart form used. For a Placidean horoscope charted on an ecliptic chart form, a small tick is placed on the outer circle at the zodiacal degree a planet occupies; the correct planetary entry is written opposite the tick inside the circle. For a Placidean horoscope charted on a houseschart form, the planets are placed in correct zodiacal sequence close to the house cusp that bears the same sign as the planet. If two house cusps bear the same sign as a planet, the planet is placed near the cusp to which the planet lies closest in the zodiac. Correct zodiacal sequence is always observed; a planet is placed on the side of the house cusp according to where it falls in the natural order of the zodiac. A planet in an intercepted sign is placed near the center of the house it occupies. Parallel lines that intersect the outer circle may be drawn on each side of a planet to indicate interception, but this is not necessary if the intercepted sign is correctly indicated by brackets outside the circle.
