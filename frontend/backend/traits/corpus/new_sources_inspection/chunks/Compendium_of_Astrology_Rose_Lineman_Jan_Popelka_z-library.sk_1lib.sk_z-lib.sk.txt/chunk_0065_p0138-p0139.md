The declination of the heavier planets changes so slowly that a correction may not be required. Most chart forms provide a space in which to enter the corrected declinations. If no space is provided, the declinations are listed on the chart form wherever there is room. The declinations may be written with the N or S following the degrees and minutes. The declinations for the Ascendant and MC are listed on the chart form with the planetary declinations. The declination of the Ascendant is the same as that of the Sun at the time the Sun occupies the same zodiacal position as the Ascendant: the declination of the MC is the same as that of the Sun at the time the Sun occupies the position of the MC. In order to determine the declination of the Ascendant, find the date in the ephemeris on which the Sun is in the same zodiacal position as the Ascendant; the declination of the Sun listed on that date is the declination of the Ascendant. If the exact position of interest is not listed, the declination entered on the date on which the Sun's position is closest to the Ascendant's position can be corrected by interpolation. The declination for the MC is found in the same way. Some ephemerides do not list the declination of the Moon's North Node. If the Node's declination is not listed, follow the same procedure used to determine the declinations for the Ascendant and MC to estimate the declination for the Node. The Part of Fortune The Part of Fortune (Pars Fortunae) is a sensitive point in the horoscope that lies a distance from the Ascendant equal to the distance the Moon lies from the Sun. The symbol for the Part of Fortune is similar to that for Earth; an X is placed inside the circle instead of the cross, which symbolizes Earth. The Part of Fortune, most widely used of the numerous Arabic Parts, is considered a fortunate point that shows by house position the area in which one's endeavors are most apt to reap success and by sign position one's approach to these endeavors. Refer to Appendix C for information about other Arabic Parts. Success indicated by the Part of Fortune does not necessarily denote material gains. Intellectual accomplishments, good health, or the vicarious success one experiences through achievements of his or her children are among the possible benefits associated with the Part of Fortune depending upon sign and house placement; it is read in context with the overall horoscope. An adversely aspected Part of Fortune, especially if it is in the sign of Pisces, may be termed the "Part of Misfortune," for little benefit is likely to manifest. To calculate the Part of Fortune, the position of the Moon is added to the position of the Ascendant; the position of the Sun is subtracted from this sum. The signs are designated by number according to their natural position in the zodiac (Aries = 1, Taurus = 2, Gemini = 3, etc.). The full circle of 12 signs may be added if necessary to carry out the subtraction. One sign may be converted to 30 degrees in order to complete calculations. The final result is reduced to lowest units. There can be no more than 12 signs, 29 degrees, or 59 minutes in the final answer (60' = 1°; 30° = 1 sign). The sign number is replaced by the usual sign designation.

### Aspects Defined

Aspects depend upon the angular distance between two planets or the distance between a planet and an important point in the horoscope such as an angle or house cusp, the Moon's Nodes or the Part of Fortune. Aspects provide the means of planetary interaction. Planets in aspect influence each other's action according to the type of aspect and the nature of the planets forming the aspect. The relationships that planets bear to each other through aspects in the natal horoscope constitute patterns of experience and development that are unique to the individual. Aspects give the astrologer a more complete picture of planetary influence in the horoscope. Calculation and Tabulation of Aspects All aspects except the parallel and the contraparallel are defined by a specified number of degrees, which separate two points in the zodiac. Parallels and contraparallels are defined by declination. Two planets that occupy the same numerical degree and direction of declination (both planets either north or south) are said to be parallel. Two planets which occupy the same numerical degree of declination with one planet north and the other south are contraparallel. The point at which an aspect is exact to the degree and minute is termed partile. An aspect that is within 1° of partile, is called an exact aspect; however, an aspect need not be exact to be effective. The number of degrees by which an aspect may vary from partile is called the orb of influence. An aspect that is not exact but still falls within the allowable orb is termed a platic aspect. The closer an aspect is to partile (the smaller the orb), the stronger its influence. Aspects fall into two general categories, major and minor. Major aspects are more powerful and influential in the horoscope than are minor aspects.
