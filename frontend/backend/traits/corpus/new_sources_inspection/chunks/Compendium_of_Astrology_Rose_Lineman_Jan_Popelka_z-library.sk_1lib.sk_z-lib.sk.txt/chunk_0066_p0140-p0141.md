156 Compendium of Astrology The orb for all minor aspects is 2°. The semi-sextile, semi-square, sesquiquadrate and quincunx are usually the only minor aspects studied in a natal horoscope. Some astrologers consider the quincunx a major aspect and weight it accordingly. The influence of the other minor aspects is slight. Nevertheless, these aspects form links between planetary energies, however weak. Frequently, the less important aspects support stronger indications in the horoscope. The faster moving planet of two in aspect is called the significator, the slower moving planet is the promittor. The significator is said to aspect the promittor; the promittor is said to be in aspect with the significator. With the exception of solar aspects, the order in which natal aspects are written is significator, aspect, promittor. It is customary to list the Sun first regardless of whether it is the significator or promittor. For example, a conjunction between the luminaries is usually written Sun conjunct Moon (O cf I)) although the Moon as the fastest moving of all the planets is the significator in any aspect it forms. When a planet is retrograde, stationary or approaching transition from direct to retrograde or vice versa, its motion may be slower than average. Check the daily motion on the birthdate to determine the correct significator if in doubt. If the significator is moving toward but has not reached the point of partile, the aspect is said to be applying (forming); if the significator is past and moving away from the point of partile, the aspect is separating (receding). An applying aspect indicates a condition, experience, action, state of mind or development that will become evident as the life of the individual progresses. A separating aspect suggests that which is a part of the individual's experience or character at birth: one's unaquired characteristics, innate knowledge or instincts, one's heredity, one's prenatal experience, perhaps one's karma. In order to calculate aspects, one usually starts with the Sun and counts the number of degrees that separate the Sun and other occupied points in the horoscope. Aspects may be counted forward or backward in the zodiac. If the direction from significator to promittor is forward in the zodiac (counterclockwise in the horoscope), the aspect is called approaching because the significator is approaching the point occupied by the planet it aspects. If the direction from significator to promittor is backward in the zodiac (clockwise in the horoscope), the aspect is termed departing because the significator is departing from (leaving) the point occupied by the promittor. Do not confuse the terms applying and separating with the terms approaching and departing. Applying and separating aspects depend upon the partile point of the aspect; approaching and departing aspects are based on the zodiacal direction from the significator to the promittor. An approaching aspect may be either applying or separating, as can a departing aspect. An approaching aspect is given a broader, more outward and social significance than a departing aspect; it is considered more overt in manifestation. A departing aspect has a more personal and subjective influence; it is given a subtle and somewhat fatalistic connotation. The Moon's aspects are calculated after the Sun's. One proceeds to do the same with each remaining planet in order of swiftest moving to slowest. The conjunction and opposition are fairly easy to recognize. Planets that lie side by side in the horoscope are conjunct if within orb; planets opposite each other in the horoscope are in opposition if within orb. For example, a planet in 5°21' Sagittarius is conjunct any planet that is posited between 27°21' Scorpio and 13° 21' Sagittarius; it is in opposition with any planet that falls between 27°21' Taurus and 13<,21' Gemini. The orb is 8° on either side of partile, which gives a 16° span of influence. A conjunction that is formed between planets in different signs is a dissociate conjunction; an opposition that occurs between planets in signs not directly opposite each other in thezodiac is a dissociate opposition. If a planet is within orb but occupies a sign different from that of the partile point, the asepct formed is called dissociate. Planets that are within a 6° orb and 60° apart form a sextile. If a planet occupies the beginning or ending degrees of a sign, it may form sextiles with planets that are one, two or three signs away. A planet in lo30' Taurus can form a sextile with any planet posited between 25°30' Aquarius (three signs from Taurus) and 7°30' Pisces (two signs from Taurus), and it can form a sextile with any planet that falls between 25<,30' Gemini (one sign from Taurus) and 7°30' Cancer (two signs from Taurus). A sextile formed from Taurus to Aquarius or Pisces is a departing sextile (backward in the natural order of the zodiac); a sextile from Taurus to Gemini or Cancer is an approaching sextile (forward in the zodiac). Planets that occupy different signs of the same quadruplicity and are not opposite each other form a square aspect if within orb. In Susan's horoscope (Chapter 13, Figure 18) Mars in 17° 59' Capricorn (a cardinal sign) squares Neptune in 18° 48' Libra (a cardinal sign). Mars is the significator (it moves faster than Neptune). The square is exact (the orb is 49', the difference between Mars' position and partile). It is applying (Mars is moving toward partile, which is 18°48' Capricorn) and departing (the direction of the aspect from the significator, Mars, to Neptune is clockwise in the horoscope: Mars is moving away from Neptune's position). A dissociate square is formed between planets within orb that are posited in signs belonging to different quadruplicities. The Sun in Susan's horoscope, placed at 7°05' Sagittarius (a mutable sign), squares Jupiter in 29°46' Aquarius (a fixed sign). The aspect isplatic; the orb is a wide 7° 19'. The aspect is separating and approaching; the Sun is moving away from the point of partile (29° 46' Scorpio); the direction of the square from the Sun (significator) to Jupiter (promittor) is forward in the zodiac and counterclockwise in the horoscope. Parallels and contraparallels are easy to identify from the declinations listed on the chart form; they can be separated by no more than 1°. Of the important minor aspects, the semi-sextile and the quincunx are theeasiest to calculate. The semi-sextile is formed by planets that are within 2° (orb) of being 30° (one full sign) apart; the quincunx is formed by planets that lie within 2° orb and are five signs apart. The semi-square and the sesquiquadrate are more difficult to recognize. It may be helpful to add 15° to the actual degrees a planet occupies and then count forward one sign or backward two signs for the semi-square and count forward four signs or backward five signs for the sesquiquadrate. For example, a planet in 5° Leo forms a semi-square with one in 20° (5° + 15°) Virgo or Gemini; the same planet can form a sesquiquadrate with a planet in 20° Sagittarius or Pisces. If this method is confusing, the actual degrees must be counted until one becomes familiar with these aspects. Most chart forms display an aspectarian similar to the one shown in Figure 19. As the aspects are calculated they are entered by symbol in the aspectarian. Zodiacal aspects are placed in the right diagonal half of the aspectarian; parallels and contraparallels are listed in the left diagonal. Aspects to the Ascendant and MC are entered in the two bottom rows of the aspectarian in the column headed by the aspecting planet. Planets that aspect the Descendant or IC form complementary aspects with the Ascendant or MC respectively; these aspects are listed according to the aspect formed with the Ascendant or MC. All aspects to the North Node and Part of Fortune, including parallels and contraparallels, are entered in the corresponding column in the row headed by the aspecting planet. Aspects to the South Node (complementary aspects of the North Node) are not recorded in the aspectarian.
