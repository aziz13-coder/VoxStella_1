### Solstice Points

Although solstice points (antiscian or mirror points) are more commonly used in horary than in natal astrology, they are worth calculating and studying. It is believed that they indicate turning points in life

It has been shown that a progressed or transiting aspect to a solstice point activates the planet to which that point relates. For example, if progressed Sun conjuncts natal Mars' solstice point, Sun/Mars activity manifests according to a Sun/Mars conjunction even though progressed Sun does not actually aspect natal Mars. Solstice points are based on the fact that the Sun occupies the same degree of declination at points equidistant from 0° Cancer (summer solstice point) and 0° Capricorn (winter solstice point). For instance, the Sun's declination when it is at 15° Taurus and at 15° Leo is the same,- both zodiacal points are 45° from 0° Cancer and 135° from 0° Capricorn. Therefore, the equidistant points share a common relationship in respect to the celestial equator on which declination is based. To calculate the solstice point of a planet or angle, subtract the degrees and minutes of its zodiacal position from 29°60'; find the proper sign in the table below. ### Fixed Stars

A planet or angle that falls within 1° orb of the position of a fixed star is emphasized in the horoscope. The strength a fixed star exerts in the horoscope depends upon that star's magnitude or degree of brightness with reference to Polaris (the polestar), a star of the second magnitude. The brightest and most influential stars are classed as first magnitude stars. Thousands of stars are visible. The following list contains twenty-six fixed stars commonly used in astrology. The positions of the stars listed were correct for 1980. To find the zodiacal longitude for any other year, multiply the number of years between 1980 and the year of interest by 50.25. The result is the required correction in seconds of longitude. If the year of interest is earlier than 1980, subtract the correction from the 1980 position. If the given year is later than 1980, add the correction to the listed position. For example, to find the position of a fixed star in 1945, multiply 35 (1980 — 1945 = 35) by 50,25. The result is 1,759 seconds of longitude, which converts to 29' 19". Subtract this correction from the 1980 position. Astrologers are not in agreement as to the significance of fixed stars in a natal horoscope. Some astrologers do not consider fixed star influence in their analyses. Each fixed star is thought to express attributes similar to those of one or more of the planets. Some fixed stars carry negative connotations, others positive. A few display both benefic and malefic properties. As a general rule a fixed star reinforces those qualities of the planet or angle it conjuncts that most closely match its own nature and those of the planet(s) that it resembles.
