DEFINITION OF
ASTROCARTOGRAPHY
Astrocartography is a captivating branch of astrology that delves into the intricate relationship
between celestial bodies and terrestrial life by combining the principles of astrology with the
tools of geographic mapping. At its core, astrocartography involves the process of plotting the
positions of the planets at the exact time and location of an individual's birth onto a map of the
Earth's surface. This mapping allows astrologers to identify specific geographic locations where
the planetary energies exert a pronounced influence, creating what are known as planetary lines
or axes.
The foundation of astrocartography lies in the belief that the planetary positions at the moment of
birth hold profound significance and influence over various aspects of an individual's life. Each
planet in the solar system is associated with specific qualities, energies, and archetypal
symbolism that are believed to shape human experience. For instance, the Sun represents the
core essence of the individual, their ego, and life purpose, while the Moon signifies emotions,
instincts, and nurturing tendencies.
By superimposing the astrological birth chart onto a map of the Earth's surface,
astrocartographers can identify the geographic regions where certain planetary energies are
accentuated or emphasized. These planetary lines are determined based on the angular
relationships between the planets and the Earth's surface, as well as the individual's unique birth
chart. For example, if a person's natal Sun is aligned with a particular longitude on the map, they
may experience a heightened sense of vitality, self-expression, and purpose when residing or
visiting that location.
Astrocartography offers a multifaceted approach to understanding the influences of planetary
energies on different areas of life. It can provide insights into various aspects of human
experience, including career opportunities, relationships, health, personal growth, and spiritual
development. For instance, individuals may consult their astrocartography maps to identify
auspicious locations for living, working, or pursuing their passions based on the alignment of
planetary lines with their natal chart.
Moreover, astrocartography is not limited to individual analysis but can also be applied to
broader geographic regions, such as cities, countries, or even continents. By examining the
planetary influences present in a particular region, astrocartographers can gain insights into the
collective energies and themes that characterize that area. This broader perspective allows for a
deeper understanding of cultural dynamics, historical events, and societal trends within different
geographic contexts.
The practical applications of astrocartography are diverse and far-reaching. Individuals may use
their astrocartography maps to make informed decisions about relocation, travel, or career
opportunities, seeking out locations where the planetary
energies align with their personal goals

BRIEF HISTORY OF
ASTROCARTOGRAPHY
The history of astrocartography is a fascinating journey through the evolution of astrology and
the exploration of human consciousness. While the precise origins of astrocartography are
difficult to pinpoint, its development can be traced back to ancient civilizations that looked to the
stars for guidance and understanding.
Ancient civilizations such as the Babylonians, Egyptians, Greeks, and Mayans were among the
first to observe the movements of celestial bodies and develop systems of astrology to interpret
their significance. These early astrologers recognized the profound influence of the planets and
stars on earthly events and human affairs, laying the groundwork for the astrological principles
that would later inform astrocartography.
The concept of mapping planetary influences onto geographic locations began to emerge in the
early 20th century with the pioneering work of astrologers such as Sepharial and A.E. Pearce.
Sepharial, a British astrologer and author, developed a system of astrological interpretation
known as "Astro
Geodetics," which sought to map the positions of the planets onto the Earth's
surface to identify areas of planetary influence.
However, it was the groundbreaking research of American astrologer Jim Lewis in the 1970s that
brought astrocartography into the mainstream. Lewis developed a systematic approach to
mapping astrological influences onto geographic locations, laying the foundation for the modern
practice of astrocartography.
Lewis coined the term "astrocartography" to describe his innovative approach, which involved
plotting the positions of the planets at the time of an individual's birth onto a map of the world.
He developed specialized charts, known as AstroCartoGraphy maps, which depicted the
planetary influences present at different locations around the globe.
Lewis's work revolutionized the field of astrology by providing a visual representation of the
astrological energies at play in different regions. His astrocartography maps offered individuals a
practical tool for understanding how planetary influences may manifest in their lives depending
on where they reside or travel.
Since Jim Lewis's pioneering efforts, astrocartography has continued to evolve and expand, with
astrologers refining techniques and methodologies for mapping planetary influences onto
geographic locations. Advances in technology have also contributed to the accessibility of
astrocartography, with the development of astrology software and online tools that enable
individuals to generate their own astrocartography maps with ease.
Today, astrocartography remains a popular and widely practiced branch of astrology, offering
valuable insights into the interplay between celestial influences and terrestrial life. Whether
used
for personal growth, career planning, or relationship compatibility, astrocartography continues to
