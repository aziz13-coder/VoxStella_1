Trade / Commerce Anchor: U.S. Protectionism and Reciprocity, 1930-1934

This local anchor supports non-energy `trade_and_commerce` hardening through
tariff escalation and trade liberalization.

External sources:
- Britannica, "Smoot-Hawley Tariff Act":
  https://www.britannica.com/topic/Smoot-Hawley-Tariff-Act
- Office of the Historian, "Protectionism in the Interwar Period":
  https://history.state.gov/milestones/1921-1936/protectionism

Anchor summary:
- Britannica says the Smoot-Hawley Tariff Act was signed on 17 June 1930,
  raised import duties, and contributed to a sharp contraction in
  international trade.
- The Office of the Historian says Smoot-Hawley became a symbol of
  beggar-thy-neighbor protectionism and that, beginning with the 1934
  Reciprocal Trade Agreements Act, the United States generally sought trade
  liberalization through bilateral or multilateral tariff reductions.
