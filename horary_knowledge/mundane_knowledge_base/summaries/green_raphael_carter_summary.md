# Green / Raphael / Carter Summary

Status: first pass

## Role In The Corpus

This is the broadest survey source in the current mundane set. It is the best book for turning a scattered traditional corpus into a practical taxonomy of public event domains, houses, planets, eclipse use, and state-level chart classes.

## Main Contributions

### 1. It defines the basic public chart framework

The book is the clearest current source for:

- Aries ingress as the principal annual state chart
- the need to inspect all four cardinal ingresses
- New Moon charts as near-term mundane windows
- eclipses as stronger, locality-bound public triggers

Primary anchors:

- pages 12-20

### 2. It gives the most systematic signification map

Green is the strongest current source for mapping:

- houses to public institutions
- planets to political, financial, diplomatic, and social outcomes
- the relation between rulers, Parliament, the people, war, trade, banks, crops, disease, and foreign affairs

Primary anchors:

- pages 22-24
- pages 37-61

### 3. It is the strongest current source for first-tier domain design

The current benchmark domains come mainly from this book's explicit treatment of:

- war and military trouble
- government instability
- cabinet change and legislative disruption
- riots, strikes, murders, conspiracies, and disorder
- revenue, taxation, commerce, banks, expenditure, and failures
- treaties, allies, foreign relations, and peace
- epidemics, mortality, and public health

Primary anchors:

- pages 45-61

### 4. It gives the strongest current locality rule for state charts

For annual state charts, the default place is the capital town.

That is currently the clearest baseline for:

- `capital_chart`
- annual framework charts
- country-level ingress handling

Primary anchors:

- pages 12-13

### 5. It preserves sign-country and sign-town traditions, but with caution

Green includes long country and town rulership lists, then weakens any naive use of them by warning that:

- some assignments need more observation
- countries may have internal subdivisions under other signs
- political and historical changes complicate older correspondences

Primary anchors:

- pages 73-88

## High-Confidence Doctrine From This Source

- Annual mundane work should start from ingress charts, not personal chart logic.
- Lunations are short windows nested inside the larger yearly framework.
- Eclipses matter most where visible and when angular.
- War, government, unrest, finance, and diplomacy are strong enough to become model families.
- Capital-based charts are the cleanest default for country-level annual work.

## Cautions

- The old ingress duration rule by Ascendant quality is preserved but not strongly endorsed.
- The old eclipse duration rule is preserved but openly questioned.
- Country and town sign correspondences are part of the doctrine, but not stable enough to become unquestioned runtime truth.

## Product Use

Use this source first when deciding:

- chart taxonomy
- domain taxonomy
- public house signification
- capital-chart default policy
- which domains are mature enough for v1

Do not use it alone to settle:

- war-event chart handling
- retrograde Mars as a hard runtime trigger
- national-chart fallback under missing event times

Those require Watters and, for deeper formal structure, Bonatti.
